# teplo — design

Source of truth for behaviour. When behaviour changes, this document changes in the same commit.
Code conventions live in [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md).

## 1. Goals

1. **Morning comfort.** Rooms reach the comfort temperature by the start of the comfort period, every day.
2. **No breaker trips.** Current on every phase stays under the input breaker limit, whatever the household switches on.
3. **Offline operation.** Control, panel and history keep working with the internet down.
4. **Convenient control.** A web panel on the LAN, usable from a phone.
5. **24/7 monitoring.** Live state, history and an event journal.
6. **Reuse.** Another house is a configuration, not a fork.

Non-goals for v1: remote access from outside the LAN (use a VPN), beyond the Telegram commands of section 9, cloud integrations, cooling, controlling the EV.

## 2. Invariants

- **Offline at runtime.** Devices are driven over the LAN. The internet serves only Telegram alerts and commands, and NTP, all best-effort; the panel on the LAN stays the complete control path when the internet is down.
- **Router-hosted.** One process on a Keenetic/Netcraze router with Entware: aarch64, Python 3.13, about 600 MB free RAM, USB flash storage.
- **Secrets outside the repository.** Device addresses and keys, tokens and passwords live in the secrets file on the router, and the office computer's broker password in the config beside its script on that computer; the repository references devices by slug.
- **Configuration is data.** Schedules, tariffs, limits and priorities live in the database, seeded by site data migrations and edited in the panel.
- **Fail-safe actuation.** While teplo is down, every floor holds at least its idle floor setpoint, and a fast heat source teplo commands holds the last setpoint teplo gave it, never more than its `max_setpoint`: teplo never leaves the office convector at the 35 °C of its app.
- **Monitor-only loads are never switched.** The washing machine, the oven, the dishwasher and group plugs are observed, nothing more.

## 3. Reference house

The first site (`sites/barsoom`) shaped the requirements.

- **Supply:** 3 phases, 15 kW allocated, C25 breaker on the pole, C20 in the house.
- **Stabilizers:** inverter, single-phase Штиль ИнСтаб IS7000 (5.5 kW output, 90–310 V input) on two phases, a third planned. At low grid voltage a stabilizer draws more input current than it delivers: about 25 A at 230 V, 30 A at 190 V, 35 A at 165 V for full load.
- **EV:** US-spec Model S 2014, single-phase charging at 10–22 A through a mobile connector, behind a stabilizer. Not controllable locally: no BLE vehicle commands, dead cellular modem.
- **Heating:** electric floors are the main heating; a fireplace is lit by hand. The second floor warms from air rising from below and the second-floor bathroom floor, and the office, which has no floor, from a 2 kW convector.

## 4. Devices

### Capabilities

The domain talks to devices through capability ports. A driver implements one or more capabilities for one protocol.

| Capability | Reads | Commands |
|---|---|---|
| `FloorThermostat` | power, setpoint, floor temperature, relay | power, setpoint |
| `MeteredPlug` | on/off, power, current, voltage | — |
| `SwitchablePlug` | as `MeteredPlug` | on/off |
| `TemperatureSensor` | temperature, optional humidity | — |
| `PhaseMeter` | current per phase | — |
| `ClimateUnit` | mode, target, its own temperature, level | — |
| `ControllableClimateUnit` | as `ClimateUnit` | power, setpoint |
| `PresenceSensor` | whether someone was there at the latest report, how long ago someone was last reported, how long the source has watched | — |
| `EvCharger` | charging state, current | current limit |

v1 drivers: Tuya thermostat, Tuya plug (read-only or switchable), Tuya heater, ESPHome temperature sensor, ESPHome phase meter, ESPHome presence (`esphome_presence`, a radar or the office computer heard like an ESPHome node), ESPHome air conditioner (`esphome_climate`, a Midea unit commanded through ESPHome's `midea` on its node).
`EvCharger` ships as a stub driver, as does any climate unit configured with the driver `unsupported`: a device can be configured with them and the panel shows it as "driver not implemented".

Every reading carries a timestamp. A device is **unavailable** after 10 minutes without a reading, counted on the monotonic clock from the moment teplo stored the reading, so setting the system clock neither hides a lost device nor makes a live one look lost; the phase meter is **stale** after 10 seconds.

### Tuya thermostats (Warmfloor ET-44W)

- Local protocol 3.3 on TCP 6668, AES with a per-device local key.
- No air sensor: the only temperature is the remote floor probe.
- Data points verified on the device:

| DP | Meaning | Values |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | power | bool |
| 2 | mode | `hold` (manual), `smart` (weekly program), `holiday` |
| 3 | relay | `heating` while the relay is closed, `warming` while the thermostat idles |
| 16 | setpoint | int, 0.1 °C, 50–400 |
| 24 | floor temperature | int, 0.1 °C |
| 121 | probably the floor limit | int, 0.1 °C, reads 450 |

The driver reads DP 3 into the thermostat state as heating, idle or, when DP 3 is missing or reads anything else, unknown, so the relay never makes a thermostat unreadable. Load management tells a heating floor from an idle one by it (section 7); nothing the heating cycle writes depends on it. DPs 104–117 are vendor-specific and unmapped; 116 is not a temperature. teplo keeps the thermostat in `hold` mode.

- **Write budget:** one state change per zone per 30 minutes. Whether the device persists every write to flash is unknown, so writes stay rare. Dry-run journals at the same cadence on its own timestamp and does not spend the budget, so a zone switched to live control writes at the next cycle. Air mode in dry-run keeps its would-be changes 30 minutes apart on a third timestamp (section 6). A live shed of load management is the one write that overrides the budget: it switches the thermostat off at once and stamps its write, so the zone's next write still waits 30 minutes; should the database refuse the stamp, load management keeps the zone shed until 30 minutes after the shed instead (section 7), and so it does with a zone shed it resumes after a restart, which cannot tell whether the stamp was stored. The write that hands a released zone back waits for the budget like any other and, like the heating cycle's writes, goes out only once its stamp is stored.
- **Write gate:** the heating cycle and the load guard run concurrently (section 5), and a cycle reads the zones' write stamps once, at its start, so a cycle still writing other zones would miss a write the guard made since. Every live thermostat write therefore goes through one write gate per zone, kept in memory and shared by the whole process: the heating cycle's writes, and load management's shed and the release that switches a thermostat back on. The gate keeps the latest write of each zone: its stamp, the moment on the monotonic clock it took the slot and the moment its stamp was stored. At the moment of a write it checks the budget and takes the slot in one step under a lock; the writer then stores the stamp and writes. Every writer tells the gate the moment on the monotonic clock it read the stamps, taken no later than the time it writes for: the heating cycle takes it when it takes its time, before it reads the wall clock and the zones, and the guard's release passes the moment it last read the loads. That order alone decides, never the wall clock. A write whose stamp was stored by that moment is one the writer read: it holds the writer exactly as the stored stamp it read does, a stamp later than the clock, as after the clock was set back, and a stamp a boost cleared since included (section 9). Any other write, one whose stamp was stored after that moment or not at all, the writer could not have read, as a guard write the heating cycle read after it took its time: it holds the zone for 30 minutes on the monotonic clock from the moment it took the slot, whatever the stamps read. A shed's slot holds the zone for 30 minutes on the monotonic clock even for a writer that read its stamp, as well as by that stamp, so a clock stepped forward after the shed, as when NTP sets a clock that ran behind through a power cut, does not let the restored zone be written sooner than 30 minutes after the shed. The heating cycle reports a write held back this way as throttled, as it does within 30 minutes of the stamp, and the guard keeps the zone shed and tries the release again the next second. The heating cycle asks the gate whether it would take a zone's write when it decides the zone, by the same rule and without taking the slot, so a write the gate holds back is throttled before the cycle asks load management for start permits and asks for none (section 6). A shed takes the slot whatever the budget. Each zone also has a lock of its own, which a writer holds from the check that its slot still holds, made after its stamp is stored, through its commands to the thermostat: the heating cycle for its write, the release for its command, and a shed, once it has taken the slot, for its command. So a write a shed overtook goes no further and is reported as throttled, and a shed that takes the slot while a write of the heating cycle is in flight switches the thermostat off right after that write, never before it. No writer holds the lock while it stores a stamp, and the heating cycle holds one zone's lock at a time, for one write, never across the cycle: a shed waits for the lock only while the heating cycle's write to that zone's thermostat is in flight, at most its power, mode and setpoint commands, a few hundred milliseconds on the LAN. A thermostat that does not answer fails that write at its first command, once the Tuya client gives up after some 11 to 16 seconds (two attempts to connect, 5 seconds each, or three to read a reply), and the cycle sends it nothing more; the shed's own command to that thermostat takes as long, and the driver already makes any command wait for one in flight to the same thermostat. A shed of another zone, a stamp being stored and a database that stays locked never hold a shed up. A boost and a restore send nothing to a thermostat and take no lock. A stamp the database refuses gives the slot back to the write before it, as nothing was written, while a write that fails after its stamp keeps it, as it took the budget. A boost frees the slot with the stamps, so the boost goes out at the next cycle as before, unless a shed took the slot less than 30 minutes before on the monotonic clock: then the boost keeps the slot and the zone's stamps, and goes out at the zone's next allowed write after the shed, never sooner than 30 minutes after it, whether it was pressed while the zone was shed or after its restore. The boost frees the slot only once the database has stored it: a boost the database refuses leaves the slot and the stamps as they were, so it frees nothing, and a write that takes the slot while the boost is being stored keeps it. A boost pressed while the heating cycle writes the zone ends as the stamps it leaves say, whichever step of the cycle's write it lands between: the next cycle reads the stamps after the boost and the gate judges its write by them, so the boost goes out at the next cycle when it cleared the stamps after the cycle stored its own, and 30 minutes after the cycle's write when the cycle stored its stamp after the boost cleared them. A restore of a zone writes nothing itself: the heating cycle's write that follows it takes the gate like any other. After a restart, before the jobs and the panel start, the gate takes back the hold of every zone with a live shed stored on its load less than 30 minutes before, whether the zone is still shed or its floor was handed back: the zone stays held for the rest of the 30 minutes after the moment stored with the shed, or for 30 minutes from the start when the clock reads earlier than that moment, as a shed resumed after a restart is (section 7), so neither a boost nor a clock set back or stepped forward lets the heating cycle write it sooner. A zone whose last write is stamped 30 minutes or more after the moment stored with its shed, as a floor the heating cycle switched back on once its hold was over and that has not heated since its hand-back, gets no hold back, whatever the clock reads at the start: its hold ended before the restart. teplo started in dry-run takes back no hold, as it writes nothing. Should the database refuse that read, the start logs `periodic_job_failed` and goes on with the stored stamps alone. Otherwise the gate starts empty, and the stored stamps decide. The dry-run stamps stay outside it. The gate is keyed (`KeyedWriteGate`): the zones' gate keys its slots, locks and history by zone slug and holds each zone for the 30-minute budget, and the budget is a parameter of the check, the reservation and a boost's clearing, so another kind of device can get a gate of its own on the same rules with an interval of its own while the zones keep theirs. The gate also remembers for 5 minutes, on the monotonic clock, the moment each write and each shed took a zone's slot, and keeps that moment when a boost frees the slot or the database refuses the stamp, as such a write may still have gone out; the watch of manual changes asks it whether teplo took a zone's slot between two moments (section 6, Manual changes). With load management in dry-run and no live shed stored on a load, as in a house where it never ran live, the guard never writes and the start takes back no hold, and every write of the heating cycle has its stamp stored before the next cycle reads the stamps, so the gate holds the heating cycle's writes exactly as their stored stamps did before it, a boost pressed at any moment and a clock set back or stepped forward included: it never holds a write the stored stamps let go.

### Tuya plugs

- Wi-Fi plugs with power metering (Tuya category `cz`), local protocol 3.5 on TCP 6668; their secrets set `protocol = '3.5'`.
- A plug's driver says whether teplo may switch it. `tuya_plug` implements `MeteredPlug` only and has no switching operation, so nothing in teplo can switch a plug that keeps it. Monitor-only plugs, such as the washing machine's, keep it: the database refuses a monitor-only device with a switching driver. `tuya_switchable_plug` implements `SwitchablePlug`: it reads the plug as `tuya_plug` does and switches the relay by writing DP 1 under the same lock as its reads, so a switch never overlaps a poll of the plug; a socket error or a reply carrying `Error` fails the write as `device_unavailable`, as it fails a read. A data migration gives the switching driver to both water heater plugs of `sites/barsoom` and to the water heater plug of `sites/example`, and only plugs with that driver are offered as the plug of a load (section 7). Only load management switches a plug.
- The driver registry builds one driver per plug, the one its device names when the plug is first used, and the poll reads a switchable plug through the driver that switches it. Asked for a switchable plug, the registry reads the device each time and refuses with `plug_not_switchable` when the device is monitor-only or its driver only reads. A plug given the switching driver while teplo runs is read through that driver once something first asks to switch it.
- The driver takes its client from the shared Tuya client builder, like the thermostat driver, so the long-lived client never switches itself to "device22" mode, which queries DP 1 only (see Power loss in section 9). tinytuya 1.20 makes that switch only for protocols 3.3 and 3.4, so for the 3.5 plugs the guard is insurance.
- A job of their own polls the plugs every 30 seconds, so a lost plug never delays the thermostats. Availability changes are journaled as for a thermostat, a plug that stays unavailable raises the device-lost alert under its own name (section 9), and the recorder samples `plug_on` (0 or 1), `plug_power` (W), `plug_current` (A) and `plug_voltage` (V) every minute.
- Data points, from the Tuya cloud model and a local status read:

| DP | Code | Meaning | Values |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `switch_1` | relay | bool |
| 18 | `cur_current` | current | int, mA |
| 19 | `cur_power` | active power | int, 0.1 W |
| 20 | `cur_voltage` | voltage | int, 0.1 V |

Not read yet: 9 `countdown`, 17 `add_ele` (energy since the previous report, 0.001 kWh), 26 `fault`, 38 `relay_status` (`memory`: the relay returns to its last state after a power cut) and the remaining vendor DPs.

### Tuya heater (Thermex Frame 2000E)

- The office convector, a 2 kW Thermex Frame 2000E Wi-Fi heater, `heater-office`: local protocol 3.5 on TCP 6668, AES with a per-device local key; its secrets table sets `protocol = '3.5'`.
- It has no power or energy data point, and its own temperature reads warm while it heats, so teplo cannot tell when its element cycles off: load management counts its rated power whenever it is on (section 7).
- Data points verified on the device; DP 1 and DP 3 were written and read back:

| DP | Meaning | Values | teplo |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | power | bool | reads, writes |
| 3 | setpoint | int, whole °C; while on it took 5, 19, 22, 24 and 35, the app's own value, and echoed each; while off it ignores a write without a reply and keeps its value | reads, writes |
| 4 | its own temperature | int, °C, read next to the heater, so it reads warm while it heats | reads |
| 7 | level | `Low` or `High`, 2000 W at `High` | reads and shows, never writes |
| 2 | probably the child lock | bool | never writes |
| 5 | probably a timer | int, 0 | never writes |

- The driver (`tuya_heater`) implements `ControllableClimateUnit`: it reads DPs 1, 3, 4 and 7 into a climate unit state, power as the mode `heat` or `off`, and writes DP 1 and DP 3 only, under the same lock as its reads, so a command never overlaps a poll. A setpoint that is not a whole degree is refused as `device_protocol_error` and nothing is written. A setpoint counts as taken only once the heater echoes it in its reply or, without an echo, reads it back; otherwise the write fails as `device_protocol_error`, «the heater did not take the setpoint 22». A level that is missing or not a word reads as none, so DP 7 never makes the heater unreadable; any other missing or mistyped data point fails the read as `device_protocol_error`, and a socket error or a reply carrying `Error` as `device_unavailable`.
- The driver registry builds one driver per climate unit, the commanding one when the device's driver commands, and the poll reads the unit through it. Asked for a commanding unit, the registry reads the device each time and refuses with `climate_unit_not_controllable` when the device is monitor-only or its driver only reads. The database refuses a monitor-only device with a commanding driver, a switchable plug's, `tuya_heater` or `esphome_climate` (`device_monitor_only_never_commanded`).
- A job of its own polls the climate units every 30 seconds and hands each reading, paired with the one before it, to the watch of changes made on a source (section 6, see Fast sources). Availability changes are journaled as for a thermostat, a unit that stays unavailable raises the device-lost alert under its own name (section 9), and the recorder samples `climate_unit_power` (0 or 1), `climate_unit_setpoint` and `climate_unit_temperature` (°C) every minute.
- **Write budget:** one change per source per `min_write_minutes`, 10 minutes by default and 5 for the convector, set per source in the admin. Every live write takes the source's own write gate (`source_write_gate`), a keyed gate of its own on the rules of the zones' gate (see Write gate above) with the source's interval, so a zone's slot and a source's slot never mix. The stamp, `last_command_at` with what teplo commanded, is stored before the command, and only a live shed of load management overrides the gate, switching the source off at once and stamping the write (section 7). No owner command clears a source's stamps, which spares the convector's relay and, later, an air conditioner's compressor: a command waits at most one interval, and the bot says when it arrives. Dry-run journals on a stamp of its own, `last_dry_run_at`.

### Local keys

A Tuya device's local key comes from the cloud once, via the Smart Life QR login used by Home Assistant: the owner reads the User Code in Smart Life (Me → Settings → Account and Security), scans a QR and confirms; the tool downloads every device with its local key and logs out. Re-pairing a device changes its key.

### ESPHome devices

ESPHome firmware publishes readings to the Mosquitto broker on the router. Configurations live in `esphome/`.

- **Phase meter:** ESP32-S3 with a U.FL antenna led outside the shed's metal door, and three SCT-013-050 clamps (1 V at 50 A) on the input phase conductors, before the stabilizers. Each clamp sits on a single conductor. The clamps share a 1.65 V bias (two 10 kΩ resistors, 10 µF to ground) and feed ADC1 pins. Reports RMS current per phase every second. In the reference house the L2 input is wired but empty, as its clamp is not on a conductor yet: L2 reports the fraction of an ampere an empty input reads, and load management estimates the phase instead (section 7).
- **Air sensors:** ESP32-C3 with SHT3x over I2C, the sensor 5–10 cm away from the board; the second floor, out of reach of the SuperMini's chip antenna, runs on an ESP32 DevKit, whose PCB antenna reaches further. Reports every 30 seconds.
- **Outdoor sensor:** DS18B20 on a 3 m cable with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up, the board indoors.
- **Link:** the air and outdoor nodes are ESP32-C3 SuperMini boards, whose crystal sits next to the antenna. At full transmit power their link fails, and at 8.5 dB the router barely hears a node a room or two away, so they transmit at 10 dB; a node may set its own power. Every node keeps its radio awake (no Wi-Fi power save) and Bluetooth off, pings the broker every 60 seconds, and restarts after 5 minutes without it, as a SuperMini can hang with its Wi-Fi associated and nothing passing.

The firmware and teplo share one MQTT contract:

- **Broker:** Mosquitto on the router, port 1883, on the LAN address only, with a user and password for each kind of client (`docs/deploy.md`): teplo takes `teplo` from the `[mqtt]` table of the secrets file (section 11), the nodes `esphome` from `esphome/secrets.yaml` (`mqtt_broker`, `mqtt_username`, `mqtt_password`), which stays out of the repository, and the office computer `pc-office` from the config beside its script (see The office computer below).
- **ACL:** the broker's ACL file lets each user publish only its own topics, so neither a node's nor the computer's credentials can drive a unit or pose as another device. `teplo` reads `teplo/#` and alone publishes commands, `teplo/+/climate/+/+/command`: the mode and target commands of an air conditioner (see ESPHome air conditioner below). `esphome` publishes its nodes' `teplo/+/status`, `teplo/+/sensor/+/state` and `teplo/+/climate/+/+/state`, reads the command topics `teplo/+/+/+/command` and `teplo/+/climate/+/+/command`, and takes part in ESPHome's IP discovery, on by default: a node reads `esphome/discover` and `esphome/ping/<node>` and answers on `esphome/discover/<node>`. `pc-office` publishes only under `teplo/teplo-pc-office/`, its will included, and reads nothing. The broker drops a publish the ACL refuses without a word to the client, so anything else a node publishes, such as the state of a radar's Bluetooth switch, goes nowhere, and teplo reads none of it; the broker logs it as `Denied PUBLISH` when it runs with `-v`. The ACL was checked on Mosquitto 1.6 and 2.1.
- **Nodes:** a node is named `teplo-` and the slug of its device, e.g. `teplo-air-kitchen`. It publishes under `teplo/<node>` without Home Assistant discovery, and its birth and will messages, `online` and `offline`, go to `teplo/<node>/status`. A device heard on another device's node names that device's slug in `Device.node`, as a radar on an air node will, `presence-office` with `air-office` heard on `teplo-air-office`; an empty `node` means the device's own node, `teplo-<slug>`, as for every device so far.
- **Readings:** every sensor publishes to `teplo/<node>/sensor/<object_id>/state` with QoS 0, not retained: a decimal string, or `nan` when the reading failed.

| Node | Device kind | Object ids | Every |
|---|---|---|---|
| `teplo-air-living-room`, `teplo-air-kitchen`, `teplo-air-hallway`, `teplo-air-second-floor` | temperature sensor | `temperature` (°C) and `humidity` (%), 1 decimal | 30 s |
| `teplo-outdoor` | temperature sensor | `temperature` (°C), 1 decimal | 30 s |
| `teplo-phase-meter` | phase meter | `l1_current`, `l2_current`, `l3_current` (A RMS), 2 decimals | 1 s |
| `teplo-pc-office`, the office computer's script | presence sensor | `presence`: 1 when there was keyboard or mouse input since its previous report, 0 otherwise | 30 s, and a 1 at once after a 0 |
| `teplo-ac-bedroom`, the bedroom air conditioner's dongle | climate unit | the climate `air_conditioner` on `teplo/<node>/climate/air_conditioner/<attribute>/state`: `mode`, `target_temperature` and `current_temperature` (°C) | 30 s, and at each change |

A node may also publish `wifi_signal` (dBm, every 60 seconds) and `uptime`, which teplo ignores. `teplo-ir-bedroom` and `teplo-spare` use the same broker but are not teplo devices.

teplo subscribes to `teplo/+/sensor/+/state`, `teplo/+/status` and `teplo/+/climate/+/+/state`, and publishes an air conditioner's commands through the same client, `MqttClient.publish`, so paho stays in `mqtt_client.py` alone:

- A node maps to the devices heard on it, each with the driver `esphome_temperature` (kind `temperature_sensor`), `esphome_phase_meter` (kind `phase_meter`), `esphome_presence` (kind `presence_sensor`) or `esphome_climate` (kind `climate_unit`): its own device, and any device that names it in `node`. teplo reads these devices from the database when it starts, so a device added later is heard after a restart.
- A reading goes to every device on its node whose driver reads its object id, so an air node carrying a radar feeds `temperature` and `humidity` to its air sensor and `presence` to the radar; its payload is read once. A climate state goes to the air conditioner on its node when its object id is `air_conditioner` (see ESPHome air conditioner below); an air conditioner reads none of its node's sensor topics. Retained messages, nodes without such a device and object ids no device of the node reads are ignored. The first message of an unknown node is logged as `esphome_node_unknown`, which points at a misspelt node name.
- A temperature sensor's `temperature` becomes a new reading, stamped when it arrives. `humidity` joins the latest reading without making it younger, so the age of a reading is always the age of its temperature; a new temperature keeps the previous humidity until the next one arrives.
- The phase meter's `l1_current`, `l2_current` and `l3_current` are the currents of phases A, B and C. Its store keeps the latest current of each phase with the moment it arrived on the monotonic clock, and the mean and maximum of each phase since they were last taken for history. The meter is stale while a phase has no current from the last 10 seconds, and the store names each such phase. The driver of a stale meter refuses to read, and the readings it gives are dated by the oldest of the three.
- Values stay decimals. `nan` records the failure `sensor_reading_failed`, and a payload that is not a number `device_protocol_error`, instead of a reading; a failed humidity also leaves the latest reading without humidity. `offline` on the status topic records `device_unavailable` for every device on the node. A broker that teplo cannot reach, that refuses its credentials or that drops the connection records `broker_unavailable` for every ESPHome device; the next connection clears that failure and no other, so a node that stays silent once the broker is back, such as one unplugged before a broker restart or one still booting when teplo connects late, is journaled as a device without a reading rather than blamed on a broker that works. As for a Tuya device, a failure keeps the last reading until it ages out, and the next reading clears it. A driver asked for a reading it no longer has raises the failure recorded for its device, with that failure's code, or `device_unavailable` when none was recorded. A failing reading is logged once, and again only after a good one.
- `presence` is 1 or 0; any other number is `device_protocol_error`, `nan` `sensor_reading_failed`. A presence source keeps in memory, on the monotonic clock, its latest report, the moment it last reported someone since teplo started, and the moment it began to watch: its first report after the start or after being unavailable. It is available while its latest report is at most 10 minutes old and arrived after its latest failure. Unlike a temperature sensor, which keeps its reading until it ages out, a presence source is unavailable at once on any failure, `offline` and its node's will, `nan` or a lost broker included, and stays so until its next report: the next connection to the broker clears the failure but not the outage, as nobody watched meanwhile. The driver gives a reading only while the source is available, with how long ago someone was reported and how long the source has watched; otherwise it raises the failure recorded, or `device_unavailable` with «no recent presence report».
- Presence sources never raise the device-lost alert: a computer that sleeps or a radar that goes silent only leaves its room's occupancy unknown (section 6, see Occupancy). Their availability is still journaled and shown.
- Without an `[mqtt]` table teplo does not connect and logs `mqtt_not_configured` once at start; the ESPHome devices stay unavailable and everything else runs as before.
- Nothing polls the sensors, the phase meter and the presence sources, so a job of their own checks their stores every 30 seconds; the climate unit poll reads an air conditioner (see below). Its first check waits a minute after the start, by when every node that is up has reported, so a restart does not journal working sensors as lost. Availability changes are journaled as for a thermostat, with the code of the recorded failure, or `device_unavailable` with the reason `no reading` when none was recorded, so the panel's journal explains every loss: a device that has not reported since the start is journaled unavailable at the first check, with the failure recorded for it, such as `broker_unavailable`, or else as `device_unavailable`. The phase meter becomes unavailable, like any device, 10 minutes after its newest current; its staleness is not journaled.
- Every minute the recorder samples `air_temperature` (°C) of every available temperature sensor, with `air_humidity` (%) when the reading has one, and the mean and maximum current of each phase since the previous sample, `phase_current_mean` and `phase_current_max` (A) under the subjects `l1`, `l2` and `l3`. Each sample starts the mean and maximum over, and an unavailable phase meter records none. Every available presence source records `presence`: 1 when it reported someone within the last minute, 0 otherwise.

### ESPHome air conditioner (Midea over the USB slot)

The bedroom's Ballu BSYI-10HN8_V4, probably a Midea OEM, gets a dongle in its USB Wi-Fi slot: a Seeed XIAO ESP32C3 running ESPHome's `midea` climate over the slot's UART at 9600 baud, the node `teplo-ac-bedroom` (`esphome/teplo-ac-bedroom.yaml`, wired as `docs/deploy.md` describes). Its page asks for a login, as it drives the unit, and the unit's beeper stays off. The dongle is ordered, not installed, so no site data seeds the unit, its heat source or its load: a device that never reports would raise the device-lost alert. Once the dongle answers, the owner adds them in the admin, the unit as a heat source of the bedroom with a load of kind `source` on its phase at the end of the shedding order, all watching only, and gives the bedroom its first comfort windows (`docs/deploy.md`, The bedroom air conditioner). `teplo-ir-bedroom` is not a teplo device: if the slot does not answer `midea`, the AC stays outside teplo, and IR returns only by a new decision.

- **State.** The climate's object id is `air_conditioner`. The node publishes `mode`, `target_temperature` and `current_temperature` on `teplo/teplo-ac-bedroom/climate/air_conditioner/<attribute>/state`, QoS 0, not retained, whenever the unit's answer changes one, and an `interval` republishes the whole climate state every 30 seconds (`publish_state()`), so the three arrive as a sensor's readings do. ESPHome leaves `current_temperature` out while the unit has not reported it. The node's `outdoor_temperature`, `power_usage`, `wifi_signal` and `uptime` count for nothing: the house's outdoor sensor decides the lockout (section 6, see Fast sources).
- **Modes.** `off`, `heat` and `cool` read as themselves, `heat_cool` and `auto` as `auto`, and every other word, `dry`, `fan_only` or one teplo has no name for, as `other` (`ClimateMode.OTHER`); a mode that is not an ASCII word is `device_protocol_error`. The state keeps the unit's own word for the modes teplo lumps together, `heat_cool`, `dry` and `fan_only` (`ClimateModeWord`), so a switch between `dry` and `fan_only` is a change made on the unit like any other (section 6, see Fast sources), while control reads only the mode.
- **Store.** A store of its own (`EsphomeClimateStore`) keeps each unit's latest mode, target and room temperature as they arrive, on the monotonic clock. A state exists once a mode and a target have arrived, and it is as old as the older of the two. `nan` for the target records `sensor_reading_failed`, and `nan` for the room temperature only leaves the state without it. Each new mode, target or room temperature clears the recorded failure; `offline` on the node's status topic and a lost broker record theirs as for every ESPHome device.
- **Driver.** `esphome_climate` implements `ControllableClimateUnit`, built by the driver registry from the device's slug and node without a secrets table. A read gives the stored state while it is at most 90 seconds old and no failure is recorded, and otherwise raises the failure with its code, or `device_unavailable` with «no recent state from the node». The climate unit poll reads it every 30 seconds like the convector, so the 10-minute availability, the device-lost alert, the samples and the watch of changes made on the unit apply as for a polled device. The poll right after a start may come before the node's first state, which takes up to 30 seconds once teplo has subscribed, so a start may journal the unit as without a connection and, at the next poll, as back. Switching the unit on publishes `heat` to `…/mode/command` and switching it off `off`; a setpoint goes to `…/target_temperature/command` with one decimal, `21.5`, and one off the 0.5 °C step is refused as `device_protocol_error` before anything goes out. A command counts as taken only once the node's state echoes it within 10 seconds, a mode and a target that both arrived after the command went out, as the node republishes its whole state when the unit takes a call, and otherwise fails as `device_protocol_error`, «the unit did not switch on» or «the unit did not take the setpoint 21.5»; the driver sends one command to a unit at a time. A state that arrived before the command never counts, however well it matches, nor does the `off` a node reports as it boots, as its target arrives as `nan`. teplo publishes with QoS 0 and queues nothing: a command while the broker is not connected fails at once as `broker_unavailable`, and one the node misses while it reboots fails for want of its echo, so the next allowed write sends it again. A command the unit already follows, which teplo sends only when its reading lags behind the unit, changes nothing there and counts only if the 30-second republish arrives within the 10 seconds.
- **Display.** A call that switches the unit from `off` to `heat`, as teplo's power-on does, turns the unit's display off 5 seconds later: the firmware's `on_control` sees the mode the call switches the unit from, as it runs before the call is applied. ESPHome sends the toggle over UART only when the unit reports light control, and otherwise only logs a warning; any other call leaves the display alone.
- **No heat check.** The unit reports its real mode, target and room temperature, so teplo needs no outlet probe: a unit that runs without warming the bedroom lets it fall to the backup threshold, and backup heat from the floor of Bathroom 2F takes over (section 6, see Backup heat).

### The office computer

`scripts/pc_presence.py` makes the owner's Windows 11 computer the office's presence source `pc-office`, heard like an ESPHome node on `teplo-pc-office`:

- **Where it runs.** One file under Python 3.12 with the standard library only, beside its config `pc_presence.toml`, which holds the broker's address and port, the user `pc-office` and its password, and the node, `teplo-pc-office` by default, and stays outside the repository. A task of the Task Scheduler starts it with `pythonw` at the owner's logon and runs it only while he is logged on, in his session: `GetLastInputInfo`, which it reads every second, gives the last keyboard or mouse input of its own session only (`docs/deploy.md`, The office computer).
- **What it reports.** `presence` on `teplo/teplo-pc-office/sensor/presence/state`, QoS 0, not retained: `1` when there was input since its previous report, `0` otherwise, every 30 seconds, and a `1` at once, within a second, when input follows a `0`. After each connection it reports at once, `1` for input within the last 30 seconds. The ingestion, availability and occupancy of presence sources above and in section 6 take it as they take a radar.
- **Its MQTT.** It speaks MQTT 3.1.1 itself, as the client `teplo-pc-office` of the user `pc-office`, with a clean session and a keepalive of 60 seconds. It publishes `online` to `teplo/teplo-pc-office/status` once connected and leaves `offline` there as its will, not retained, as the nodes do. It pings the broker every 60 seconds and takes a missing reply within 10 seconds for a lost connection. Ctrl+C in a console publishes `offline` before it disconnects. A computer that sleeps, loses its network or is switched off simply drops the connection: the broker publishes the will at once when Windows closes the socket, as at a logoff or shutdown, and otherwise after 1.5 keepalives, 90 seconds, and the office reads unknown (section 6, see Occupancy). It reconnects on its own, 1 second after a failure and then doubling the wait up to 30 seconds, after a resume as after a broker restart.
- **One copy per session.** It takes the named mutex `Local\teplo-pc-presence` before it reads its config, and a second copy in the same session exits at once with code 3: two copies would keep taking each other's broker session under the one client id. A config it cannot use ends it with code 2 and says why.
- **Its log.** `pc_presence.log` beside the script, rotated at 1 MB with one older file, and the console when there is one: the connection, each change of presence, every failed connect and lost connection with its reason, never the password. A broker's refusal reads by its meaning, e.g. `connect failed: not authorized`, which Mosquitto answers to a wrong user name or password.
- **Checks.** CI runs its tests, which drive the packets, the reporting rule, the backoff, the config and the loop against stubs, under Python 3.13 with the rest and again under 3.12; ruff checks it as Python 3.12 and mypy checks it with teplo.

## 5. Architecture

### Process

One process on the router runs an asyncio runtime:

- device drivers and the MQTT client feed a **state store** per device kind (latest readings and availability): jobs poll the Tuya devices, the thermostats, the plugs and the climate units each in a job of its own every 30 seconds, the MQTT client writes the ESPHome readings as they arrive, an air conditioner's climate state into a store its driver reads when the climate unit poll asks, and a job checks the ESPHome stores of the sensors, the phase meter and the presence sources for availability changes (section 4); the thermostat poll then hands each thermostat's reading, paired with the one before it, to the watch of manual changes, and the climate unit poll each unit's reading to the watch of changes made on a source (section 6);
- the MQTT client runs in paho's network thread from just after the start journal entry until after the last sample flush. It subscribes again on every connection and reconnects on its own, 1 second after a failure and then doubling the wait up to 30 seconds. A message that fails in handling is logged and dropped, so it never ends the thread. It also publishes the commands of an air conditioner, from the thread of the source cycle or of the load guard that sends them, while it is connected (section 4);
- a job tracks each room's occupancy every 30 seconds from its presence sources and keeps it in an occupancy book in memory, which the room intents read (section 6, see Occupancy);
- control loops read the state stores, decide, and send commands through drivers: the heating cycle every minute (section 6), the source cycle every minute in a job of its own, started 30 seconds after the heating cycle, so a failing heating cycle never skips the fast heat sources (section 6, see Fast sources), and the load guard every second (section 7);
- a recorder samples every available device each minute and writes the history to SQLite in batches every 5 minutes. The metrics are `floor_temperature`, `floor_setpoint` and `thermostat_power` for a thermostat, `plug_on`, `plug_power`, `plug_current` and `plug_voltage` for a plug, `air_temperature` and `air_humidity` for a temperature sensor, `climate_unit_power`, `climate_unit_setpoint` and `climate_unit_temperature` for a climate unit, `presence` for a presence source, and `phase_current_mean` and `phase_current_max` for each phase of the phase meter;
- an alert job checks the state stores every minute and queues alerts, a report job checks every minute for an outage before the start until it is reported, and a third job sends the queue to Telegram (section 9);
- the owner's command listener, when the secrets file switches it on (section 9), runs in a thread of its own, like paho's, from just after the MQTT client starts until the process ends: it long-polls the Telegram bot and runs each command's use cases in that thread with fresh database connections, so a poll never holds a worker of the jobs' thread pool;
- uvicorn serves the Django ASGI app (panel and API) from the same loop;
- use cases are synchronous: each periodic job runs its use case in a worker thread (`asyncio.to_thread`) with fresh database connections, and Django runs the panel's views in its own thread, so blocking device and ORM calls stay off the loop;
- jobs run concurrently: in-memory state (state stores, sample buffer, alert book, alert outbox, the occupancy book, the heating cycle's control book with the fire detector's readings, the sources' outdoor lockouts and the would-be targets the source cycle last journaled, under its own commands and under occupancy, the load guard's guard book, the zones' write gate and the sources' write gate with the moments of their recent writes) is guarded by locks, the Tuya drivers serialize calls to each device, every live thermostat write takes its zone's write gate and sends its commands under the zone's lock and every live command to a heat source takes the source's write gate and its lock (section 4), and the MQTT thread writes the sensor stores while jobs and the panel read them.

```mermaid
    mqtt["MQTT client"] -->|"ESPHome readings, as they arrive"| store
    store --> loops["Control loops<br/>every minute, the guard every second"]
```

### Modules

Bounded contexts are Django apps laid out in the altyn-backend style described in `CLAUDE.md` (domain policies, use cases, models, repositories, services, drivers, views):

- **house**: floors and rooms, the topology the other contexts place their zones and devices in; it imports no other context.
- **climate**: zones, room comfort and room intents (guests, overrides), heat sources (the fireplace, the office convector) and the source cycle that commands the fast ones, schedules, regulation, optimal start and learning.
- **presence**: each room's occupancy, from the presence sources placed in it; it has no models and keeps occupancy in memory.
- **power**: power cuts and their causes, loads, phase limits, the load guard: shedding, phase map learning and EV detection.
- **devices**: registry, capabilities, drivers, state store.
- **tariffs**: tariff intervals.
- **telemetry**: history samples, event journal, retention (planned).
- **notifications**: Telegram alerts and the owner's commands from the same chat.
- **system**: trusted clock, dry-run, health.
- **panel**: web pages and API.

Control policies in `climate/domain`, `power/domain` and `presence/domain` import nothing from Django, Tuya or MQTT; AST architecture tests keep it that way.

## 6. Heating control

### Zones

- **Air zone:** has an air sensor; teplo switches the floor to hold the room air at the target.
- **Floor zone:** no air sensor; teplo sets the thermostat's floor setpoint from the schedule and the thermostat regulates itself.
- A zone has one or more heat sources ordered by priority. v1 uses floor thermostats only; a heat pump can become the first source later with the floor as backup.
- A zone's air sensor is a `temperature_sensor` device linked to the zone. The climate settings name the outdoor sensor; a room without a zone of its own names its air sensor in its room climate, as the bedroom does. Both come from the site data and are edited in the admin; the heating cycle reads the outdoor sensor for optimal start and a room's air sensor for its backup heat (see Backup heat), and the panel shows their readings, the room's on its card (section 10).
- A zone belongs to a room (`Zone.room`), set by the site data; a room holds several zones, as the open space does, or none. A zone takes its comfort windows and air targets from its room's climate (see Schedules and targets), and the panel groups the zones by floor and room (section 10); load management does not read the room.

### Control paths

Air control ships in dry-run: gates edited in the admin keep the thermostats on their floor-mode writes until the owner switches air control on, zone by zone. Every minute the heating cycle picks one path per zone:

| Path | Zone | The thermostat follows |
|---|---|---|
| floor mode | a floor zone, or an air zone whose air sensor is unavailable | floor mode |
| air mode | an air zone with `air_control_live` on and its air sensor available | air mode |
| air mode in dry-run | an air zone with `air_control_live` off and its air sensor available | floor mode, while air mode journals what it would do |

`air_control_live` is off by default. `TEPLO_DRY_RUN` and a zone's `live_control` gate every thermostat write on every path as before: a write they hold back is journaled as `zone_command_issued` with `dry_run` set, on its own 30-minute stamp. Backup heat has a gate of its own per room, `backup_live` on the room's climate, off by default. On every path, boost and backup heat included, load management holds a shed zone, and a write that starts a zone waits for a permit (see Shed zones and starts); with its gate on, a zone changed on its thermostat is left alone for 2 hours (see Manual changes).

The owner switches the gates in the admin. A zone's `air_control_live` is ticked in the zone list or on the zone's page, one air zone at a time, once the dry-run journal (see Dry-run journal) shows what air mode would do, while the zone's card (section 10) names the path that drives it and its air target; the next cycle runs the zone in air mode, and its writes take the zone's write budget like any other. Unticking it hands the thermostat back to floor mode at the zone's next allowed write. A room's `backup_live` is ticked on its room climate the same way. The zone's page shows the boost, the write stamps and the end of a hold by hand (`manual_until`) read-only, as the heating cycle, the boost and the watch of manual changes write them: saving the page never posts back the values it was opened with, which could cancel a boost or a hold or let a second write follow within 30 minutes. While the zone's load is shed live, the page also shows the zone's slug and thermostat read-only (section 7).

### Schedules and targets

A room holds the comfort: its weekly comfort windows and its comfort and night air targets (`RoomClimate`) apply to all its zones; each period selects a mode with a target. Each zone keeps its own pre-heat lead and optimal start, so the open space has one schedule and one target while each of its floors warms up on its own lead. A zone without a room, or whose room has no room climate, keeps the windows and targets stored on the zone. A room's windows are rows of its own: the site data copied them from the zone that held them, and its migration stops before it changes anything when that zone is gone or renamed while the room holds other zones, which would take an empty schedule, and names the zone to give back in the admin. Every zone keeps its own rows, unread while its room holds the comfort, so deleting a zone never takes the room's schedule with it, and a release that predates rooms, editing a zone's windows on the new schema, never changes the room's. The owner edits a room's comfort windows on the panel's schedule page and its targets on the room's card of the settings page (section 10).

| Mode | Air target | Default period |
|---|---|---|
| comfort | 22.0 °C | 06:30–23:30 every day |
| night | 19.5 °C | the rest |

Pre-heat, the lead before a comfort period, aims at the comfort target. **Boost** holds a zone at its boost floor setpoint for 2 hours. Guests and the owner's 2-hour override give a room one target for a while (see Room intent).

### Room intent

What a room needs now is decided by one pure function, `resolve_room_intent`, which the heating cycle, the source cycle, the panel and the zone-off alert read alike through `RoomIntentReader`. The first rule that applies wins:

1. **Override**: the owner's target for the room, for 2 hours from the last press or command.
2. **Occupancy**, in a room whose gate `follows_occupancy` is on and that has no heated floor, while its occupancy is known and no guests stay in it, on any clock: the comfort target while someone is there and the night target once nobody has been there for `vacant_after_minutes`, with no end (see Occupancy).
3. **Untrusted clock**: the comfort target, as the zones hold comfort themselves (see Fallbacks).
4. **Guests**, in a guest room: the comfort target until the guests leave at `guests_end_hour` (14:00 by default) after the coming night, today's when guests are switched on before 06:00 and tomorrow's otherwise, or the day after when that hour has already passed, as an end hour of 02:00 does at 03:00; each further night, up to 3, moves the end a day later. Guests are switched on only on a trusted clock, as their end is reckoned from it, and end early on any clock.
5. **Schedule**: the comfort target inside one of the room's comfort windows, until it ends.
6. **Setback**: the night target until the next comfort window.

An override counts only while it ends within 2 hours from now and guests only within 4 days, trusted clock or not, as a boost does (section 9), so neither outlives a clock set back after a reboot.

Only an override and guests force a target, each over the target the room holds without it: guests over the schedule's, and an override over the guests', the schedule's or, on an untrusted clock, the comfort target. Occupancy forces nothing: the floors read only the forced targets, which are reckoned without occupancy, so an override in a room that follows occupancy is forced over the target the room would hold without it. Otherwise each zone follows its own mode on its own lead, as without a room intent, so the open space can read setback while the hallway already warms up on its longer lead. A forced target therefore moves a zone only the way it moves the room: a target above the room's target beneath it raises a zone whose own target is lower, a target below it lowers a zone whose own target is higher, and every other zone keeps its own. Guests apply first and an override over them. So «+» pressed at 08:30, while the open space reads setback at 19.5 °C and the hallway already pre-heats for a window at 12:00, sets 20.0 °C, which keeps the hallway's pre-heat and starts the living room's and the kitchen's at once; and «−» pressed while guests hold the open space at 22.0 °C through the night sets 21.5 °C, which lowers its floors.

- **Floor mode** takes a forced target through the three floor setpoints only. Raising a zone moves its floor one setpoint up, from night on a trusted clock to the comfort setpoint and from comfort or pre-heat to the boost setpoint; lowering it moves the floor to the night setpoint. So guests keep the open space's floors at comfort through the night, an override of 22.5 °C in a comfort window of 22.0 °C raises them to their boost setpoint, and one of 21.5 °C lowers them to the night setpoint, in the window and over guests at night alike. A boost and heating switched off win over a forced target.
- **Air mode** regulates to the target the forced targets leave the zone as it does to the scheduled one, and the zone reads comfort for a target above its night air target and night otherwise.
- **Learning** stops while a target is forced, as during a boost, while the room has external heat, a fireplace marked lit or, with its gate on, a fire the detector sees, and while the zone is held by hand: no morning starts or goes on, and no early stop is watched (see Learning).
- **Backup heat** applies last, so it still raises a floor that an override lowered, but never a floor held by hand.
- **Held by hand.** A zone changed on its thermostat, with the gate `manual_hold_live` on, takes no write at all for 2 hours, whatever its room's intent, guests, an override and backup heat included (see Manual changes).
- **Reaching the floor.** Guests and overrides are the owner's intents, like a boost, and have no gate of their own: they reach a floor only through its gates, `TEPLO_DRY_RUN` and `live_control`, at the zone's next allowed write within its 30-minute budget, with a start permit when the write starts the zone, never while load management holds the zone shed (see Shed zones and starts) and never while it is held by hand. Unlike a boost they leave the write stamps alone. The panel names the setpoint the floor takes and the earliest moment the budget allows the write, or says that load management holds the floor off (section 10).

An **adjustable** room takes overrides: one of its zones is an air zone with an air sensor, as in the open space, or a heat source teplo commands heats it, a heater or an air conditioner with its device, as the convector heats the office. The panel steps its target by 0.5 °C, each press setting the override for 2 hours from then, within 10–30 °C, and «Вернуть расписание» clears it. The same use case takes the steps the Telegram commands give: 1 °C warmer, and at least to the comfort target, 1 °C cooler, or a target set outright. So «холодно в офисе» at the office's setback of 19.0 °C sets its comfort, 21.5 °C, for 2 hours. A room without an air reading or such a source, as a bathroom, takes no override and keeps boost on its floor.

**Fast sources.** A room intent's target is also the target its fast heat sources hold: see Fast sources below.

### Occupancy

A room holding presence sources, devices of kind `presence_sensor` placed in it, has an occupancy, decided by a pure function (`presence/domain/occupancy.py`) from what its available sources report:

- **Occupied** while any of its available sources reported someone within the room's `vacant_after_minutes`, 20 by default and 5–240 on its room climate. A sighting made before a source's outage counts once the source reports again.
- **Vacant** once at least one of its sources is available and has watched for `vacant_after_minutes` without anyone. A source watches from its first report after teplo starts or after it was unavailable, so the room reads unknown rather than vacant right after a restart, or after its source comes back, until it has watched that long or someone is reported, and a restart never drops an occupied room to its night target.
- **Unknown** when none of its sources is available, as a computer that sleeps or is off, or a radar that is silent or reports `nan`, or while the available ones have not yet watched for `vacant_after_minutes`. Every source, the office computer and a radar alike, can both occupy and clear its room.

`TrackOccupancyUseCase` decides every room with presence sources every 30 seconds and keeps the result in memory in the occupancy book, which the room intents read; it writes nothing to the database. Each change is journaled as `room_occupancy_changed` with the occupancy before it and `live`, whether the room follows it, «Кабинет: никого» with «Наблюдение: отопление идёт по расписанию» below while the gate is off; a restart finds every room unknown and journals nothing for it.

The gate is the room climate's `follows_occupancy`, off by default and ticked in the admin. Until it is ticked occupancy is only shown and journaled, and the source cycle journals what occupancy would give the room's sources (see Dry-run journal). With it on and the occupancy known, the room's intent follows occupancy inside and outside its comfort windows (see Room intent), and only its fast sources take it: the office convector holds 22 °C for the office's comfort target of 21.5 °C while someone is there and 19 °C, its night target, once nobody has been there for 20 minutes, never off. An override and staying guests still win, and −/+ and the chat's commands step from the target occupancy gives. Unknown occupancy returns the room to its windows. A floor never follows occupancy, as a floor takes hours under a 30-minute write budget: the admin refuses the gate in a room with a zone, and the room intent ignores it there anyway.

With the gate on, a computer that sleeps or is switched off makes the office unknown, so the office follows its windows again: 21.5 °C, the convector at 22 °C, inside 13:00–23:45, even with nobody there. A computer whose sleep timer is shorter than `vacant_after_minutes` therefore never reads vacant: it sleeps, unknown, before the 20 minutes without input are up. The options are a longer sleep timer, a shorter `vacant_after_minutes`, deleting the office's comfort windows, so that unknown means its night target and only presence brings comfort, or accepting the reheat; the owner picks one before he ticks the gate (`docs/deploy.md`, Presence in the office).

### Floor mode

Every zone carries a comfort, a night and a boost floor setpoint for floor mode, and an idle floor setpoint and a floor limit for air mode (see Actuation). A floor zone always runs in floor mode; an air zone runs in floor mode while its air sensor is unavailable or its air control is off.

- comfort period and pre-heat: comfort floor setpoint;
- night: night floor setpoint;
- boost: boost floor setpoint;
- clock untrusted: comfort floor setpoint;
- a forced room target: one setpoint up or down from the schedule's (see Room intent).

| Zone | Comfort | Night | Boost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living room, Kitchen, Hallway | 28 °C | 22 °C | 30 °C |
| Bathroom 2F | 30 °C | 22 °C | 30 °C |
| Bathroom 1F | 18 °C | 18 °C | 30 °C |

Pre-heat in floor mode starts a fixed lead before the comfort period, 3 hours by default and configurable per zone.

```mermaid
    state "Pre-heat · comfort setpoint" as preheat
    state "Comfort · comfort setpoint" as comfort
    night --> preheat: lead before the comfort period
    preheat --> comfort: comfort period starts
    comfort --> night: comfort period ends
```

### Regulation of air zones

In air mode a zone is in one of two air states, heat or idle, decided every minute from its air sensor.

- The air target is the comfort air target in the comfort period and in pre-heat, and the night air target at night.
- Heat starts when air < target − start offset (0.3 °C, climate settings).
- Heat stops when air ≥ target − early-stop offset. The offset is learned per zone (see Learning) and starts at the initial early-stop offset of the climate settings, 0.3 °C.
- The early-stop offset in effect is at most the start offset less the minimum band (0.2 °C, climate settings, validated between 0.1 and 1.0 °C), so heat stops at least the band above where it starts, whatever the zone has learned. Without it the two thresholds met when both offsets were 0.3 °C, as on the first night of dry-run at a night target of 19.5 °C, or crossed once a learned offset grew past the start offset, and either way the zone turned heat on and off every 30 minutes, paced only by the write budget. The band clamps the early-stop offset in effect, never the owner's start offset: with the defaults heat starts below target − 0.3 °C and stops at target − 0.1 °C, 19.2 and 19.4 °C at night. A start offset narrower than the band would stop heat above the target, so the settings page and the admin refuse a start offset below the band, whichever of the two is edited (section 10).
- In between, the zone keeps its air state. A zone entering air mode, when its gate is switched on, its air sensor returns, its shed ends or teplo starts, starts idle unless the start condition holds.
- Minimum 30 minutes between state changes of a zone: a change that needs a thermostat write waits for the zone's write budget (section 4), and for a start permit when it starts the zone (see Shed zones and starts), and until the write goes out, or is journaled while writes are held back, the zone keeps its previous state. A change that needs no write, as during a boost or while the zone is disabled, takes effect at once.
- teplo keeps the air state of each zone in memory.

### Actuation

| Zone state | Thermostat power | Thermostat setpoint |
|---|---|---|
| heat | on | floor limit (default 30 °C) |
| idle | on | idle floor setpoint (default 18 °C) |
| boost | on | boost floor setpoint, whatever the air state |
| shed | off | unchanged |
| disabled | off | unchanged |

Idle is a raised floor minimum rather than off, so a dead controller leaves the house at no less than 18 °C on the floor. Load management, not the heating cycle, switches a shed zone off (section 7), and the heating cycle leaves it off until the shed ends (see Shed zones and starts). The idle floor setpoint and the floor limit are set per zone and the air targets per room (see Schedules and targets), validated like the other setpoints; the reference house gives its air zones 18 °C and 30 °C, and the open space 22.0 °C and 19.5 °C.

### Optimal start

teplo starts heating early enough to reach the comfort target at the start of the comfort period.

- Lead time = dead time + max(0, comfort air target − current air) / heat-up rate. The rate is the one learned for the 5 °C bucket of the current outdoor reading, or else the one of the nearest learned bucket, the colder one on a tie.
- Until a zone has 3 learned mornings (climate settings), or without an air or an outdoor reading, the lead is the zone's fixed pre-heat lead, 3 hours by default. Every lead is capped at 4 hours (climate settings) and is never below the dead time.
- Optimal start drives the pre-heat only of an air zone in air mode. Floor zones, and air zones in floor mode or in air mode in dry-run, keep their fixed lead.
- The lead is computed every minute until the pre-heat starts; from then the start is held until the comfort period begins, so a room that warms faster than planned stays in pre-heat. teplo keeps the start in memory: a cycle after a restart computes it again from the room as it is, and a sensor that drops out and returns finds it held.
- In air mode in dry-run, floor mode pre-heats on the fixed lead and warms the air the lead is computed from, which would push a later would-be start further back every minute. So the would-be start is held from the start of floor mode's pre-heat as well, on the lead computed then, before the floor has warmed the air; the would-be pre-heat still begins at its own start.
- An untrusted clock pauses optimal start along with the schedules: no new start is held, and a start already held stays held, so a minute of doubt about the clock in the middle of a pre-heat neither ends it nor sets a new start from the room it has warmed.

### Learning

Each zone keeps what it has learned in its own row (`ZoneLearning`), written at most once per stop and once per morning, so the flash sees few writes. Progress within a stop or a morning stays in memory: a restart loses only the stop or the morning in progress. A row that cannot be written, as while the database stays locked, loses that stop or that morning: the failure is logged as `zone_early_stop_offset_save_failed` or `zone_morning_save_failed` and the write is not retried; the zone goes on with what it had learned before. The admin lists the rows read-only: the early-stop offset and beside it the one in effect, which the minimum band may hold lower (see Regulation of air zones), the dead time, the heat-up rate of each outdoor bucket and the count of learned mornings; its one action resets them (see Reset).

- **Early-stop offset.** When heat stops in air mode and the write of the idle floor setpoint goes out live, teplo watches the air: the peak is the highest reading of the next 2 hours, or of the time until heat starts again if that comes sooner. The stop asks for the offset in effect when heat stopped + (peak − target), and the learned offset moves toward it by an exponential moving average with alpha 0.3, rounded to 0.01 °C and kept within 0 and 1.5 °C. The offset in effect is the one the minimum band left (see Regulation of air zones), so while the band holds it below the learned offset, the learned offset still settles on how far the room coasts after a stop instead of growing with every stop the band moved, and it takes effect once a wider start offset leaves room for it. A stop teaches only under the target the zone last chose to heat for: a stop caused by a falling target, as at the end of the comfort period, teaches nothing, and neither does a stop in dry-run, because the floor keeps heating. A boost, a forced room target (see Room intent), external heat in the zone's room (see External heat), a lost air sensor, the zone leaving live air mode, a shed, a hold by hand (see Manual changes) or an untrusted clock drops the watch.
- **Mornings.** A morning is the pre-heat that drives an air zone, observed from its first minute: the zone goes from night to pre-heat, both seen on a trusted clock, with its air sensor and the outdoor sensor available, its thermostat written live and set to the pre-heat's target in that minute or already holding it, heating enabled, no boost and no forced room target. A pre-heat whose first write waits for the write budget or a start permit, fails or finds the thermostat unavailable or the zone shed is not a morning, as the floor would start heating later than the pre-heat. Today's floor-mode pre-heat with its fixed lead counts as a morning: the floor is the heat source either way, at its comfort setpoint in floor mode and at its floor limit while air mode heats. So a zone learns while it runs in floor mode or in air mode in dry-run, and has its mornings when air control is switched on. The cycle samples the air every minute. The dead time lasts from the start of the pre-heat until the air has risen 0.1 °C; the rate is the rise after the dead time divided by the hours until the air reaches the comfort target or, short of it, until the comfort period starts. In air mode the pre-heat stops heating at the comfort target less the early-stop offset and lets the air coast the rest of the way, so the first minute it goes idle, its idle floor setpoint written or already set, ends the morning as if the air had reached the target, and the idle minutes after it do not lower the rate; a pre-heat that starts idle is not a morning. The floor-mode pre-heat heats until the comfort period and keeps the rule above. A morning is dropped when a sensor is lost, the thermostat becomes unavailable or a write to it fails, the zone is shed or held by hand, a boost starts, its room's target is forced or its room has external heat, the zone stops heating on schedule or the pre-heat ends before the comfort period, but not when a write waits for the write budget or a start permit, as the thermostat holds what it was last given meanwhile. One whose rise after the dead time is 0.2 °C or less teaches nothing, and neither does one faster than its cap (see Morning cap). A learned morning moves the zone's dead time, and the rate of the bucket of the outdoor reading taken when the pre-heat started, by an exponential moving average with alpha 0.3, and counts the learned mornings. A pre-heat already running when teplo starts, or when its clock becomes trusted, is not a morning, and neither is one whose floor was not at its night setting when it began: the night before a morning counts only once the zone's thermostat took the night's target on a trusted clock, as written, already held or journaled while writes are held back. A night minute whose write waits for the write budget or a start permit, fails, or finds the thermostat unavailable or the zone shed leaves the thermostat with what it held before, so it counts as the minute before it did: as night after a night the thermostat took, and not after a pre-heat, a comfort period or a comfort target an untrusted clock held. So when an untrusted clock held the comfort floor setpoint, the first trusted minute of night found the thermostat unavailable and the air sensor returns in a minute whose write budget lets the pre-heat's write go out, no morning starts on a floor that has been heating all along. teplo's first cycle has no air reading yet, as the sensors report every 30 seconds and their retained messages are ignored, so it runs an air zone in floor mode, whose fixed lead may still read night while air mode's own pre-heat runs; a zone that enters air mode from night into pre-heat therefore starts a morning only when the pre-heat's write goes out in that minute, not when its thermostat already holds the pre-heat's target, which it may have taken before teplo started. A comfort start has one morning at most: the zone keeps in memory the comfort start of the last pre-heat its thermostat took on a trusted clock (set to the pre-heat's target, already holding it, or with the write journaled while writes are held back), on whichever path and whether a morning started then or not, and starts no morning for that comfort start again. So when the air sensor drops out of air mode's pre-heat and floor mode's shorter fixed lead reads night, the held pre-heat that resumes when the sensor returns, on a floor that has been heating since before, starts no morning, whether a morning had started for it and was dropped or its first write had waited for the write budget or a start permit and a later one went out; a pre-heat whose start the zone missed because the air sensor was lost then still becomes a morning when the sensor returns and the pre-heat's write goes out, as long as its thermostat has not taken a pre-heat for that comfort start.
- **External heat.** A fire in the fireplace warms the open space far faster than its floors, so a morning or a stop it overlaps would teach the zones a room that warms itself. The fireplace is a heat source of the open space without a device (`HeatSource`, kind `fireplace`), and teplo learns nothing in a room with external heat: none of its zones starts or goes on with a morning or watches an early stop (see Room intent). A room has external heat while its fireplace is marked lit, and, once the fireplace's own gate `live` is ticked, while the detector sees a fire.
  - **Mark.** «Топлю камин» on the room's card or in the chat (section 9) marks the room's fireplace, or every fireplace of the house from the chat, lit for 4 hours from the press (`lit_until`), and pressing it again marks 4 hours from then; «Камин погас» ends the mark at once. A mark counts only while it ends within 4 hours from now, trusted clock or not, as a boost does (section 9), so it never outlives a clock set back after a reboot; «Камин погас» clears every stored mark, one beyond those 4 hours included, so a clock that catches up does not bring it back. The mark needs no gate and no trusted clock. Both are journaled, `fireplace_marked` with the end of the mark and `fireplace_mark_cleared` only when a mark counted, each with its fireplace as the subject, its name, its room's name and where it came from, the panel or Telegram.
  - **Detector.** The heating cycle watches the air of each room with a fireplace, the air sensors of the room's zones and of its room climate, and keeps in memory each sensor's readings of the last 20 minutes, one a minute; a restart starts the watch over. A sensor read in the current minute with 15 readings or more gets a rate, the slope of a least-squares line through them (`statistics.linear_regression`), in °C an hour to 0.01. A fire starts when at least two sensors of the room rise at 1.5 °C/h or more, or the only one of a room with a single sensor; a sensor that is unavailable gives no rate. A fire ends once every rate read stays at 0.3 °C/h or less for 30 minutes, a minute without a rate starting the calm over, or 6 hours after it started. Each start and end is journaled, `external_heat_started` with every sensor's rate and `external_heat_ended` with its reason, `calm` or `timeout`, the room as the subject and its name, whether the gate is on or not: until the owner ticks the fireplace's `live` in the admin, the detector only journals, and the entries carry `live` false. With the gate on, a fire the detector sees withholds learning as a mark does. There is no veto for cooking: the journal shows first whether the oven is taken for a fire.
- **Morning cap.** A morning faster than its cap teaches nothing: the cap is 3 times the heat-up rate the zone learned for the outdoor bucket of that morning, or else for the nearest learned bucket, the colder one on a tie, as optimal start picks it (see Optimal start), and at least 1.5 °C/h, which a zone that learned nothing gets. Such a morning is journaled as `zone_morning_rejected` with its rate, the cap, the outdoor bucket and the dead time, the zone as the subject and its name, and nothing is stored; the cap needs no gate.
- **Reset.** «Reset learning» («Сбросить обучение») in the admin's list of zone learning resets what each selected zone learned: the early-stop offset, the dead time, the rates and the learned mornings, so the zone starts over on its fixed lead and its initial early-stop offset. A zone that learned nothing is left alone. Each reset is journaled as `zone_learning_reset` with the values the zone had learned, the zone as the subject and its name. A reset counts itself in the row (`resets`), and the heating cycle stores what a zone learned only into the row it read at the start of its minute: a morning or a stop a cycle finishes after a reset it did not read is not stored, logged as `zone_learning_reset_meanwhile`, so a cycle in flight cannot save the old learning back. The next cycle reads the new count and drops the zone's morning and early-stop watch in progress, as they began before the reset; no other thread touches the cycle's memory.

### Backup heat

A room without heating of its own can name a backup zone whose floor warms it: the bedroom has no heated floor and warms from below and from Bathroom 2F, its backup zone.

- Backup heat turns on when the room's air sensor reads below `backup_below`, 19.0 °C, and goes off at `backup_below` plus `backup_hysteresis`, 0.3 °C, or more; in between it keeps its state. The air sensor, the threshold, the hysteresis and the backup zone come from the room's climate. A lost sensor, or no backup zone, leaves backup heat off and the zone on its schedule.
- With the room's `backup_live` on, the backup zone heats at least at its comfort floor setpoint, on whichever path drives it. Backup heat only raises: a zone already at its comfort or boost setpoint keeps it, and a disabled zone stays off. Its writes take the zone's write budget like any other.
- teplo keeps the state of each room's backup heat in memory; it starts off.
- With a backup zone set, the database keeps `backup_below` at least 0.5 °C below the room's night target, or backup heat would fight the night target; the settings page and the admin refuse anything closer.
- Earlier releases called it the second-floor guard and kept it in the climate settings; site migration 0009 moved its sensor, zone, threshold, hysteresis and gate to the bedroom unchanged.

### Shed zones and starts

Load management (section 7) reaches the heating cycle in two ways, on every path, boost and backup heat included.

- **A shed zone is held.** While load management has a zone shed live, the cycle neither writes to its thermostat, which the shed switched off, nor stamps or journals a write for it, and air mode in dry-run journals nothing for it either. The zone keeps no air state meanwhile, and a morning or an early-stop watch in progress is dropped, as the floor is off; the comfort start it last pre-heated for and, in air mode in dry-run, the would-be thermostat target last journaled stay in memory. The restore releases the hold, and the zone's target returns at its next allowed write: as the shed stamped its write, or was held until 30 minutes after the shed when the database refused the stamp or teplo restarted meanwhile, never sooner than 30 minutes after the shed, a boost pressed meanwhile included (section 4). A zone whose `live_control` is switched off while it is shed stays held until load management switches its thermostat back on at that next allowed write (section 7). A dry-run shed switched nothing off and holds nothing.
- **A start needs a permit.** A write starts a zone when it switches the thermostat on, or raises its setpoint above the floor temperature while the thermostat does not heat: its relay idles or, with the relay unknown, its setpoint is at or below the floor temperature. Every minute the cycle first decides all zones on what their sensors and thermostats read and on whether each zone's write gate would take its write (section 4), asks load management for a permit for each write that would start a zone, served most important first by the priority of its load, and then runs zone by zone as it did before load management: each zone is decided again at its turn on its air and its thermostat as they read then, and on whether it is shed or its write gate holds its write then, so a poll that lands while an earlier zone is being written counts for the zones after it. A write that starts a zone at its turn goes with the answer to the cycle's request; a start the cycle did not ask for, as when the thermostat read otherwise when it asked, waits for the next cycle when its permit would be live and is written as before otherwise. A refused start writes nothing and keeps the write budget, so the zone asks again at the next cycle; meanwhile the zone keeps its state, as when a write waits for the budget. The permit is live only for a zone whose writes are live and whose load is live with `load_management_live` on; otherwise the cycle writes as before and journals `start_would_wait_dry_run` when the permit would have been refused and the write at the zone's turn still starts it (section 7). A zone that is not a load writes as before.

### Manual changes

A change made on a wall thermostat or in the vendor app holds its zone for 2 hours, so the owner's setpoint, power or mode stays instead of being reverted at the zone's next allowed write. The hold has a gate of its own, `manual_hold_live` in the climate settings, off by default: until the owner ticks it in the admin, each change is only journaled with the moment its hold would end, and teplo reverts it at the zone's next allowed write as before.

- **Detection.** After each thermostat poll, every 30 seconds, teplo compares each thermostat's reading with the one before it, when both polls read the device and the first reading was stored at most 90 seconds before the second. A poll that fails in between breaks the pair, so a thermostat that stays off the network across a poll, as through a power cut, and comes back changed is not taken for the owner, and neither is the first reading after teplo starts. A thermostat that reboots and answers again before the next poll, as after a dip of its phase, cannot be told from the owner by its readings: one that comes back switched off holds its floor off for 2 hours once the gate is on, with the zone-off alert quiet as for any hold (section 9), so the journal before the gate is ticked shows whether that happens (see Open questions). A pair is a manual change when the power, the mode or the setpoint differs, never the relay or the floor temperature, and no write of teplo took the zone's write slot from 60 seconds before the first poll until the watch has read the zones' stamps after the second (section 4): a write takes its slot, stores its stamp, which may wait up to 20 seconds on a locked database, and then sends its commands, which wait for a read already in flight, so a poll after the slot can still read the old state. The slots of sheds count as writes too, so neither teplo's writes nor its sheds are taken for the owner; the price is that a change of the owner less than about a minute and a half after a write of teplo to the same zone is not seen.
- **Hold.** With the gate on, a manual change stores the end of its hold on the zone (`manual_until`), 2 hours from the poll that saw it, and a change seen again during the hold moves the end. The hold is stored only while the zone's write stamp is still the one the watch read: a write, a shed or a boost of teplo that stamped the zone since, as one waiting on a locked database while the watch read the zones, leaves the change neither held nor journaled, logged as `zone_manual_hold_overtaken`, so no hold outlives the shed or the write that came after the change. A hold counts only while it ends within 2 hours from now, trusted clock or not, as a boost does (section 9). The heating cycle reports a held zone as `hands_off` and leaves it alone on every path: no write, no stamp, no dry-run entry and no start permit, whatever the schedule, guests, an override or backup heat ask for. Like a shed zone, it keeps no air state, drops a morning or an early-stop watch in progress and learns nothing, and keeps the comfort start it last pre-heated for and the would-be target last journaled. When the hold ends, the zone's target returns at its next allowed write. Unticking the gate ends every hold at once; a hold whose 2 hours have not passed applies again should the gate be ticked again.
- **Sheds and boosts win.** A live shed of load management still switches a held floor off at once (section 7); its stamp ends the hold, and so does the shed's record on its load, which load management stores within a minute even when the database refused the stamp, so the floor returns to the heating cycle after its restore. No hold is stored while load management keeps the floor shed, so a watch that read the stamps before the shed cannot store its hold after it. A boost, the owner's newer intent, ends the hold too and goes out at the next cycle as before.
- **Journal.** Each manual change is journaled as `zone_manual_change` with the zone as its subject, its name, the power, the mode and the setpoint before and after, whichever changed, the end of the hold and `dry_run` while the gate is off (section 10). A hold the database refuses is logged as `zone_manual_hold_failed`, and that change is neither held nor journaled; the other zones' changes still are.
- The heating cycle reads the zones at the start of its minute, so a cycle that read them before a hold was stored may still write the zone in that minute and revert the change once: when its stamp lands before the hold, the change is not held; when it lands after, the hold keeps the floor at teplo's target until it ends.

### Fast sources

A fast source warms a room in minutes where a floor takes hours: a `HeatSource` of kind `heater`, as the office convector, or `air_conditioner`, with the climate unit teplo commands (section 4). The room's intent gives its target (see Room intent), the comfort target from the start of a comfort window, with no lead, and the setback outside them, or, in a room that follows occupancy, the comfort target while someone is there and the setback once nobody has been there for `vacant_after_minutes` (see Occupancy); the office holds 21.5 °C in its windows, 13:00–23:45 every day as the site data seeds them, and 19.0 °C otherwise. No room has both a floor and a source teplo commands: the admin refuses a heater or an air conditioner in a room with a zone, and the fireplace is only observed (see External heat).

The source cycle (`RunSourceCycleUseCase`) runs every minute in a job of its own, 30 seconds after the heating cycle (section 5). It reads the sources, the rooms' intents and the outdoor sensor in one transaction, then decides each room with sources (`climate/domain/fast_source.py`):

- **Choice.** The room's source is the first by priority whose unit reads, in any mode but `cool`, not shed by load management (section 7) and not locked out (see Outdoors). A source teplo switched on, as `commanded_power` stored with its last command says, that is no longer the room's choice, as when it is locked out, is switched off at its next allowed write; a source the owner switched on is left alone.
- **Modes.** A unit in `cool` is the owner's until he switches it off, as cooling is a non-goal: teplo never chooses it, never writes it and gives it no pending caption. A mode set on the unit, between two modes it runs in, dry and fan only among them, is a change made on it like a new setpoint (see Changes on the unit): it holds the room for 2 hours. After the hold, a unit in `auto` or another mode, such as dry or fan only, is taken back to `heat` at its next allowed write, or switched off if that is teplo's target, while a unit in `cool` stays the owner's. The convector reads only `heat` or `off`, so none of this changes what it is given.
- **Outdoors.** A source with `min_outdoor`, −15.0 °C for an air conditioner, is locked out below it, and while teplo has no outdoor reading from the last hour: the outdoor sensor's latest temperature counts for an hour after it arrived, on the monotonic clock. A locked-out source is released once the reading is at or above `min_outdoor` plus 1.0 °C, −14.0 °C, so a reading that hovers around −15 °C does not switch the source every minute. The lockout is kept in memory and logged as `source_outdoor_lockout` when it changes, and teplo starts unlocked, so after a restart at −14.5 °C the source runs. A source without `min_outdoor`, as the convector, is never locked out.
- **Power.** Without an air sensor in the room, as in the office until block C, the source stays on and its own thermostat holds the target. With one, it switches on below the target less 0.3 °C and off once the air reaches the target plus 1.0 °C, and keeps its state in between, so it writes rarely.
- **Setpoint.** teplo gives the unit the target plus the source's `setpoint_bias` (0.0 °C by default), rounded half up to the device's step, 1 °C for a heater and 0.5 °C for an air conditioner, and at most its `max_setpoint` (24.0 °C by default) rounded down to the step, and at least 5 °C, or 17 °C for an air conditioner, the lowest ESPHome's `midea` takes, as the admin refuses an air conditioner's `max_setpoint` below 17.0 °C, so `max_setpoint` always bounds what teplo gives: the office's 21.5 °C gives the convector 22 °C and its 19.0 °C gives 19 °C. teplo never gives a unit its maximum, so a teplo that is down leaves a source at the last target it gave it (section 2).
- **Writes.** A change writes the power and then the setpoint, only what differs from what the unit reads, as the convector ignores a setpoint while it is off. A unit teplo switches on gets its setpoint at once, 0.4 s later on the convector, and one that does not take it is switched off again and the command fails, so teplo never leaves a unit it switched on at a setpoint it did not give; teplo dying between the two writes is the one gap. An air conditioner is switched on in `heat`, and one taken back to `heat` from another mode gets its setpoint the same way. A write starts the source only when it switches it on: a unit that is on counts as drawing whatever its setpoint (section 7), so a setpoint change starts nothing, and neither does taking an air conditioner that is on back to `heat`. A start asks load management for a permit like a floor's (section 7), and a refused one writes nothing and keeps the write budget, so the source asks again at the next cycle; once the start's command has gone out, failed or not, the cycle tells load management, which counts the source from then until a poll reads it (section 7, see Estimated phase). Each write takes the source's write gate with its interval and stores `last_command_at` with what it commanded, `commanded_power` and `commanded_setpoint`, before the command (section 4).
- **Gates.** A command reaches the unit only while `TEPLO_DRY_RUN` is off and the source's `live`, `load_management_live` and the `live` of the source's load are all on, so 2 kW on the phase without a clamp never runs under teplo without live load management (section 7); the admin's list of heat sources names the gates that hold its commands back. The load guard reads the gates at most once a minute (section 7), so a live command also waits, as `waiting`, until the guard serves the source's load live itself: in the minute after the last gate is ticked the cycle reads them on while the guard would still take its start as a dry-run one, granted without a look at the phase, and shed it only in dry-run. Otherwise the cycle journals `source_command_issued` with `dry_run`, once for each new would-be target and at most once per `min_write_minutes` on the stamp `last_dry_run_at`, stored first so that a stamp the database refuses holds the entry back: «Конвектор: кабинет: поставил бы 19°» in the evening and «… поставил бы 22°» at 13:00. The would-be target last journaled stays in memory, so after a restart the next cycle journals it again.
- **Held.** While load management holds a source shed the cycle writes nothing to it (`shed`); a source whose unit is unread is `device_unavailable`.
- **Changes on the unit.** After each poll of the climate units teplo compares each unit's reading with the one before it, as for the thermostats (see Manual changes): two consecutive polls that both read the unit at most 90 seconds apart, with no write or shed of teplo in the source's write gate from 60 seconds before the first poll until the watch has read the sources' stamps after the second, and a hold is stored only while the source's write stamp is still the one the watch read, as for a zone. The power, the setpoint and the mode between two modes the unit runs in count, the mode by the unit's own word, so `dry` to `fan_only` counts though both read as `other`, never the level or the unit's own temperature, and a unit switched on or off counts as a change of power alone; a unit that reboots between two polls and comes back changed is taken for the owner, as a thermostat is. While the source's commands reach it, a change holds the source for 2 hours (`manual_until`), counted on any clock like a boost, and is journaled as `source_manual_change`, «Конвектор: кабинет: изменили на приборе: 22° → 25°, teplo не трогает до 18:40» or «Кондиционер: спальня: изменили на приборе: режим обогрев → осушение, teplo не трогает до 18:40»; otherwise it is only journaled, «… teplo не трогал бы до 18:40». A hold on any source of a room holds the room: the cycle writes to none of its sources until it ends (`hands_off`). A live shed still switches a held source off and its stamp ends the hold, and no hold is stored while load management keeps the source shed.
- **Failures.** A command that fails is journaled as `source_command_failed` with its error code; the stamp it stored keeps the write budget. A source that fails on the database is logged as `source_cycle_failed` and reports failed for that minute while the other sources still run. A room with sources but no room climate is skipped and logged once as `source_room_without_climate`.

### Dry-run journal

The dry-run paths journal what they would do only when it changes, never every minute, as each journal entry is written to flash:

| Entry | Written when | Payload |
|---|---|---|
| `air_control_dry_run` | the would-be thermostat target of a zone in air mode in dry-run changes | air, target, schedule mode, would-be state, the would-be state the zone followed until then, would-be power and setpoint, the start offset and the early-stop offset in effect, and in pre-heat the lead in minutes |
| `optimal_start_dry_run` | the start of the pre-heat a zone in air mode in dry-run would use is held, once per morning: when that pre-heat begins, or when floor mode's pre-heat begins if that comes first | lead in minutes, would-be start, start of the fixed lead |
| `backup_heat_dry_run` | a room's backup heat, with its gate off, would turn on or off | the room, its air, whether backup heat would be on, the comfort floor setpoint it would hold |
| `occupancy_dry_run` | a fast source of a room with presence sources and `follows_occupancy` off would get another target under occupancy than it gets, once per change | the source, its name, the room and its name, the occupancy, the would-be power and setpoint |

Entries the second-floor guard of earlier releases wrote as `second_floor_guard_dry_run` keep their wording. The source cycle reckons, for a room whose occupancy is known and whose gate is off, the targets its sources would get under occupancy and journals each one that differs from what the source gets that minute, whether the source's commands are live or not: «Конвектор: кабинет: по присутствию держал бы 19°» in the office's window while nobody is there, or «… держал бы 22°» at 23:45 while someone still is. The would-be target last journaled stays in memory per source and is forgotten once the source gets that target anyway; a minute in which an override counts, guests stay or the occupancy is unknown, or in which occupancy would give the source no target at all, as while the room is held by hand, load management holds the source shed or its unit gives no reading, journals nothing and keeps it, so the entry does not repeat when the override, the hold, the shed or the outage ends. Writes, permits, holds and the source's own entries are the same as without occupancy. A zone in air mode in dry-run runs the air regulation and the optimal start of air mode on its own would-be state and follows it as live air mode would, so the journal shows what live air control would really write. A change of the would-be state that leaves the would-be thermostat target as it was, as during a boost or while heating is off, is followed at once without an entry, as live air mode follows it without a write; a change of the would-be thermostat target is journaled. These entries keep 30 minutes apart on a stamp of their own, `last_air_dry_run_at`, apart from the write stamp and `last_dry_run_at`; a change held back, or made while the thermostat is unavailable, keeps the previous would-be state, as a held-back write keeps the live one. With a boost from 07:00 to 09:00, a would-be stop of heat at 08:50 is followed without an entry, and the idle floor setpoint is journaled at 09:00, when the boost ends and live air mode would write it. A boost clears the stamp as it clears the write stamp. A stamp that cannot be stored, as while the database stays locked, is logged as `zone_air_dry_run_stamp_failed` and holds the change back as the 30 minutes do: no entry, the previous would-be state kept, and the next cycle tries again, so no entry goes out without its stamp. teplo keeps the would-be thermostat target last journaled, the would-be state the zone follows and the would-be start in memory, so after a restart the next cycles journal them again. A zone that leaves air mode in dry-run, as while its air sensor is lost, takes as its would-be thermostat target what its thermostat follows on the path that drives it meanwhile, floor mode's target or live air mode's, once the thermostat holds it, as written, already set or journaled while writes are held back, and none while it does not. So the first entry after the zone returns compares what air mode would write with what the thermostat really holds: with the sensor lost from 07:10 to 07:40 in the comfort period, floor mode keeps the floor at its comfort setpoint of 28 °C, and air mode's 30 °C for heat is journaled when the sensor returns, though it was the would-be target journaled before the loss, while the boost setpoint floor mode wrote during a boost is not journaled again. Each entry names the zone as its subject, and the panel's journal puts it in words (section 10).

### Fallbacks

- Air sensor unavailable: the zone runs in floor mode until the sensor returns, then enters air mode again.
- Clock untrusted: schedules pause and every zone holds its comfort target, the comfort floor setpoint in floor mode and the comfort air target in air mode, unless an override that counts gives its room another target (see Room intent). A room that follows occupancy keeps following it, as occupancy needs no clock; staying guests still win over it.
- Manual changes made on a thermostat or in a vendor app hold the zone for 2 hours while `manual_hold_live` is on, and are reverted at the zone's next allowed write while it is off (see Manual changes).
- Database failure: the zones run one by one, and a zone that fails on the database, as when its write stamp cannot be stored while the database stays locked, is logged as `zone_cycle_failed` and reports failed for that minute, without a `zone_command_failed` entry, keeping in memory what it held the minute before; the other zones still run and the commands the cycle sent are journaled, and so is a `start_would_wait_dry_run` entry whose zone then fails, as load management notes each waiting episode once. The write stamp is stored before the thermostat is written, or the held-back write journaled, so a stamp that fails holds the write back, gives the zone's write slot back (section 4) and no write goes out unstamped. What a zone learned is stored after the write, and a failure to store it is only logged; a would-be stamp that fails holds its dry-run entry back (see Learning and Dry-run journal). A zone deleted in the admin after the cycle read the zones fails its turn the same way once a repository no longer finds it, as when what it learned is stored: it is logged as `zone_cycle_failed` with the error `zone not found` and reports failed, while the other zones still run and the cycle's entries are journaled, a command its turn sent before included. The cycle reads the climate settings, the zones, what they have learned and the rooms' climate with their intents in one database transaction before backup heat or any zone moves, so a room card saved meanwhile reaches a zone's targets and its room's intent alike, as the card's targets serve both, and a read that fails ends that minute with nothing changed and the next cycle journals a backup heat change in full; a zone decided again at its turn keeps the intent read at the start of the minute.

## 7. Load management

Load management ships in dry-run. A guard checks the phases every second and decides what to shed and restore; until the owner switches it on in the admin, load by load, it only journals those decisions (see Gates).

### Inputs

- Current per phase every second from the phase meter (section 4), and for a phase without a clamp an estimate from the loads on it (see Estimated phase).
- The latest state of each load's thermostat, plug or climate unit, as the polls leave it in the state stores (section 4).
- The loads and the load settings, seeded by the site data and edited on the panel's settings page, their gates in the admin (section 10).

### Loads

A load is a zone's floor, a plug or a fast heat source that teplo may switch off to relieve a phase: in the reference house the two water heaters, the five floors and the office convector. A load has a slug, a name, what it controls (its zone, its plug by device slug, or its heat source, exactly one of them, as the database keeps it), a unique priority from 1, the phase the owner set (`l1`, `l2`, `l3` or `unknown`), the nominal current the owner set, if any, and a `live` gate of its own. Passive learning adds the learned phase, the learned current and the number of votes behind them (see Phase map learning).

- **Effective phase:** the owner's phase, else the learned one, else unknown.
- **Effective current:** the nominal current, else the learned one, else, for a heat source, its rated current, the source's `rated_watts` over 230 V to a tenth of an ampere, else the default load current of the load settings, 6.0 A. The convector's 2000 W count 8.7 A at either level until its power at `Low` is measured, in the estimate, the start permits and the restores alike.

Learned values never override what the owner set. Only a plug whose driver can switch it can be a load (section 4), and only a heat source with its device; monitor-only plugs and the EV are never loads.

A load **draws** while its plug is on and draws more than 0.5 A, while its thermostat is powered and its relay heats, or while its climate unit is on, whatever its own temperature: the convector reports no power, and its temperature is read next to it and reads warm while it heats, so it sits around its setpoint while its element still heats; with the relay unknown, a powered thermostat set above its floor temperature draws. A load whose device is unavailable, or whose last poll failed after its latest reading, neither draws nor is chosen, so the guard sends no shed or restore that would wait out the timeouts of a device that does not answer.

### Gates

A command reaches a device only when `TEPLO_DRY_RUN` is off, `load_management_live` in the load settings is on and the load's `live` is on, and, for a zone, the zone's `live_control` is on, as for every thermostat write, and for a heat source the source's own `live`. `load_management_live` and every load's `live` are off by default. Otherwise the guard takes the same decision and journals it as a `*_dry_run` entry (see Journal), once, when it is taken: no entry repeats every second or every minute, as each one is written to flash.

### Thresholds per phase

| Threshold | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| start ceiling | 20 A | a load starts only if the predicted current stays at or below it |
| shed | above 24 A for 3 s | shed loads on that phase, one per second, until ≤ 20 A |
| restore | ≤ 18 A for 2 min | return one shed load per minute |

The load settings hold them with the other limits: one shed a second, one restore a minute, starts 30 seconds apart, the meter lost after 60 seconds stale, and the default load current. The database keeps the restore threshold at or below the start ceiling and the start ceiling below the shed threshold.

Rationale: a C25 breaker holds 1.13 × In (28 A) for an hour and trips within an hour at 1.45 × In (36 A); real trips came from overloads around 37 A. Seconds of reaction time are enough.

### Shedding priority

The loads' priorities order the list; loads are shed from the top and returned from the bottom. Default, as `sites/barsoom` seeds it with both water heaters at a nominal 6.0 A, Water heater 2F, Bathroom 1F and the office convector on L2 and every other phase unknown:

1. Water heater 1F
2. Water heater 2F
3. Bathroom 1F
4. Hallway
5. Kitchen
6. Bathroom 2F
7. Living room
8. Office convector («Конвектор: кабинет»)

A load the site data adds later joins at the end, at the highest priority taken plus one, without renumbering the owner's order, so the convector is shed after the living room until the owner moves it on the settings page.

Loads on the overloaded phase are shed first, then loads of unknown phase. Monitor-only loads and the EV are never in the list.

### The guard

A job runs the guard every second, in two steps. The first watches the phases, learns phases, spots EV charging and journals what it sees; the second sheds and restores. So the overload entry is on flash before the shed it causes. Runs never overlap: a device write inside a run delays the next check.

The guard keeps its state in memory: the watch of each phase, the last 2 minutes of per-second phase currents, the loads' latest readings, the sheds, the last start permit and when each load last got a live one, the estimate of a phase without a clamp, the loads waiting to start and what learning and EV detection have seen. It reads the phase meter's store and the thermostat and plug stores every second, and the loads, the load settings, the zones under live control and the phase meter's slug at most once a minute, so an edit in the admin reaches it within a minute and an ordinary second neither reads nor writes the database. It writes only on events: an overload and its end, a shed, a restore, an override, a failed command, a learned phase and an EV charge. A shed never waits for the database: the guard switches the device first, then stamps a zone's write, and stores a live shed or its end once the second's commands are sent. A zone's shed waits only for a write of the heating cycle to the same thermostat already in flight, and the release only for the same, under the zone's lock (section 4). The release that switches a zone's thermostat back on respects the write budget, so, as the heating cycle does, it takes the zone's write gate (section 4) and stores the write's stamp before it writes (see Restoring). When the database cannot take a write, the guard goes on: it keeps the shed in memory and stores it a minute later, logs a shed zone's stamp it could not store and keeps that zone shed until 30 minutes after the shed, and leaves a learned phase for the load's next vote. When it cannot read, as when looking up a plug's switching driver finds the database locked, or cannot store a release's stamp, that command goes no further and reaches no device: the guard logs `load_command_failed` with the database's error, journals nothing, counts the load as unavailable until its device is read again, as after a failed write, and goes on with the rest of the second, keeping the commands it has already sent. Hold periods run on the monotonic clock.

### Overload and shedding

The guard follows each phase in two views. The live view is the measured current less the relief of live sheds: a live shed relieves its phase until the meter delivers a current of that phase read at least 5 seconds after the shed, the time its drop takes to show, so a meter whose messages lag or stop for a few seconds after a shed never makes the guard shed again on a current from before the drop. The dry-run view also takes off every dry-run shed until it is restored, as nothing really drops, so it shows the phase as a guard with every load live would see it. Live decisions use the live view and only live loads, dry-run decisions the dry-run view and only the loads that are not live: a dry-run shed never stops the shedding of a live load and never makes room for one. A phase whose live view stays above the shed threshold for 3 seconds is overloaded, and `phase_overload` is journaled with the phase and its current. While the overloaded phase stays above the start ceiling in a view, the guard sheds one load a second for it in that view: the first by priority among the drawing loads not yet shed whose effective phase is that phase, then among the drawing loads of unknown phase, which an estimated phase passes over (see Estimated phase). The overload ends once the live view has stayed at or below the restore threshold for 2 minutes, and `phase_calm` is journaled; the dry-run view keeps an overload of its own, which only paces the dry-run sheds and restores. So in a house where no load is live, nothing relieves the live view and `phase_overload` and `phase_calm` follow the real current: a dry-run shed that would have brought the phase under the restore threshold returns its load on the dry-run view's calm, journaled as `load_restored_dry_run`, while `phase_calm` waits until the measured current itself has stayed at or below the restore threshold for 2 minutes. A phase without a fresh current restarts its shed holds and keeps the overload; its calm holds, the restore threshold's in each view and the alert's, only pause while it is silent and count on from where they stood once it reports again, unless it stayed silent for the meter-lost time of 60 seconds, which restarts them. So a meter whose link drops out for a few seconds every minute still lets a calm phase end its overload and return its loads, once its readings add up to 2 minutes at or below the restore threshold. A current counts once: the holds move on only with a newer reading, so one reading above the shed threshold followed by a stall of the meter's messages is not taken for 3 seconds above it. A phase that stays above the shed threshold for a minute also raises the phase overload alert (section 9).

Shedding a plug switches it off; only a plug that is on draws, so only such a plug is shed. Shedding a zone switches its thermostat off at once, overriding the 30-minute write budget (section 4) and a hold by hand (section 6, Manual changes), and stamps the write like any other, a stamp that also ends the hold, as does the shed's record on its load, which a stamp the database refused does not keep from being stored, so the floor returns to its schedule after the restore; a zone whose stamp the database refuses is neither restored nor released before 30 minutes after the shed, so its next write waits as long as after a stamped one. A zone shed resumed after a restart is held the same way, counted from the moment stored with it, as the restart cannot tell whether its stamp was stored. A floor handed back less than 30 minutes before a restart keeps the rest of that hold too: the write gate takes it back at the start (section 4), so a boost pressed after the restart, or a clock set at it, never lets the heating cycle switch the floor on sooner than 30 minutes after the shed. The heating cycle holds a shed zone and writes nothing to it; once the zone is restored, its own target comes back through the heating cycle at its next allowed write, never sooner than 30 minutes after the shed (section 6). A live shed is stored on the load: when it happened, why, the phase it was shed for, the device it switched off, the zone's thermostat or the plug, and, for a plug, whether the plug was on, so a restart keeps it and restores it by the same rule. A shed stored without its device, as by the release before the one that added it, which runs on while a deploy migrates the database before the restart, is taken as switched off on the device the load controls, as the migration that added the device took the sheds stored before it; otherwise it would read as pointed at another device and be released to none. The guard keeps the same in memory from the moment of the shed, so a shed still waiting for the database (see The guard) knows its device too. A restore and a release switch back on the device the shed switched off, never the one the load or its zone names by then: a load or a zone pointed at another plug or thermostat in the admin, while the shed is stored or still waits in memory, does not redirect them. Such a shed is released as when its gate is switched off (see Restoring), journaled with the cause `released`: the plug the shed switched off goes back on at once, and the thermostat at the zone's next allowed write, as the heating cycle will drive the zone's new thermostat; until then the zone stays shed and held, and a calm phase does not restore it. The admin refuses to delete a load while it is shed live, and with it the zone or the plug it controls, and keeps the load's slug, its kind and what it controls read-only meanwhile, and on the zone's page the zone's slug and thermostat, as the guard follows a shed by the load's slug and the heating cycle holds a zone by its slug: so a load is never left switched off without a shed to restore it. The locks follow the shed the database has stored and stay a convenience: what teplo switches back on is the device the shed switched off. A floor handed back after an overload keeps its shed stored, marked as handed back, until it heats again (see Phase meter lost); it is no longer shed, so the locks and the refusal to delete leave it. A dry-run shed lives in memory only. A write that fails is journaled as `load_command_failed` with its error code, and the load counts as unavailable until its device is read again, so the next second sheds the next load. A plug may still have switched off, as when it took the command and only its replies were lost, so the first reading of a plug after its failed shed decides: a plug that reads off is taken as shed then, journaled as `load_shed` and stored with the moment of the failed write, and comes back by the restore path like any other shed; a plug that still reads on stays unshed. A load pointed at another plug before that reading is taken as shed at once, journaled and stored the same way, as its readings now come from the other plug and cannot tell whether the one the shed tried switched off; it is then released like any shed whose load was pointed at another device, so the plug the shed tried is switched back on, which changes nothing if the shed never took.

### Restoring

Each view restores its own sheds. Once a phase has stayed at or below the restore threshold for 2 minutes in a view, the guard returns one of the view's sheds a minute, the most important first, each only if the phase's current in that view plus the load's effective current stays at or below the start ceiling, on every phase for a load of unknown phase. A load whose device is unavailable is passed over, and a load shed for no known phase waits until every phase has calmed; nothing is restored while the meter is stale, and while it is lost only floors come back (see Phase meter lost). Restoring a plug switches it back on only if teplo switched it off, and needs a start permit (see Starting loads): the guard first checks that the start gate would grant it, so a restore that waits for its permit reads nothing from the database, and takes the permit only once it has found the plug's switching driver. Restoring a zone ends its shed and the heating cycle's hold; the thermostat gets the zone's target from the heating cycle at its next allowed write, which needs a start permit when it switches the thermostat back on (see Starting loads). A restore whose write fails is journaled as `load_command_failed`, once per shed however often it fails again, and the load counts as unavailable until its device is read again: the guard tries it again only after a newer reading, and only when no other shed can return in its place, so the next shed returns a minute later even while a plug that cannot be switched still answers its polls. Once it is the last shed left, or the only one with room, it is tried again at the restore pace, once a minute, after each newer reading of its device, a plug's poll every 30 seconds, and journaled no more.

- **Owner override.** An owner who switches a shed plug back on by hand wins: the first poll that reads it on drops the shed, journaled as `load_shed_overridden`, and the guard does not shed that plug again until the overload on its phase ends. A restore whose write switched the plug on but whose reply was lost reads the same way: the restore is journaled as failed, the next poll reads the plug on, and the shed is dropped as an owner override, `load_shed_overridden` and all, with the plug kept from the sheds of the overload still going on its phase, if any.
- **Gate switched off.** Switching a load's `live`, `load_management_live` or a zone's `live_control` off releases the load's live shed through the restore path when the guard next reads the loads, and `load_restored` names the release as its cause; so does pointing the load or its zone at another device (see Overload and shedding). teplo hands a load back as it found it: a plug teplo switched off is switched back on at once, whatever its gates. A zone whose writes stay live returns to its schedule at its next allowed write, as after a restore. A zone whose `live_control` is off is no longer written by the heating cycle, so the guard keeps it shed and held until its next allowed write, 30 minutes after the shed's write and after the zone's last write, as the zone's write gate (section 4) counts it, then stamps the write and switches its thermostat back on: a shed overrides the write budget, a release and a restore respect it. Until the gate allows the write, the guard tries the release every second, in memory, and journals nothing. As with the heating cycle's writes, the stamp is stored before the thermostat is written, so no release goes out unstamped and the zone's next write, the heating cycle's once `live_control` is back on, waits 30 minutes after it. A stamp the database refuses holds the release back as a failed lookup does (see The guard): nothing reaches the thermostat, and the zone stays shed and held until the release is tried again, or until its `live_control` is back on and the guard restores it as any shed. A shed resumed after a restart counts those 30 minutes from the moment stored with it, or from the restart when the clock reads earlier than that moment. A release that fails is tried again only after a newer reading and 30 minutes after the attempt. One whose write fails is journaled like a failed restore; its stamp was stored before the write, so the attempt took the write budget, and the stamp, which the guard reads with the loads, keeps that deadline across a restart and, once `live_control` is back on and the guard restores the zone, holds the heating cycle's next write as long. Switching a load live drops its dry-run shed the same way, without a write.
- **`TEPLO_DRY_RUN`** holds back every write, so teplo started in dry-run neither resumes nor restores a live shed stored on a load; the shed stays stored until teplo runs live again. Meanwhile the plug or the thermostat the shed switched off stays off, and the zone-off alert reports such a thermostat (section 9).

### Starting loads

A start needs a permit from the guard's start gate: a fresh phase meter, at least 30 seconds since the last permit granted, and the measured current of the load's phase, or its estimate (see Estimated phase), plus its effective current at or below the start ceiling, on every phase for a load of unknown phase. While the meter is lost, a floor starts on the terms of limp mode instead (see Phase meter lost). The gate judges by the guard's state as its last second left it: the meter, the loads' readings and the measured phase currents. A live start is judged on the measured current alone: a live shed makes room for it once the meter shows its drop, never on the relief the live view counts while the shed settles (see Overload and shedding), as the meter reports every second and its drop may already show, which the relief would count a second time; a dry-run shed never makes room for it. A dry-run start is judged on the measured current less the dry-run sheds still standing, as the dry-run view takes them, without the relief of settling live sheds. Three kinds of start ask for a permit: the guard's restores of plugs, the heating cycle's writes that start a zone and the source cycle's writes that start a heat source (section 6). Both cycles take their permits from the one gate, first come first served, `start_interval_seconds` apart, and the starts one cycle asks for at once are served most important first; as permits are 30 seconds apart and the cycle runs every minute, one zone starts a minute. A write the zone's write gate holds back, as within 30 minutes of a live shed on the monotonic clock, asks for no permit (section 4), so it never takes the start of the minute from a zone that can start. A refused start writes nothing and keeps the zone's write budget, so the zone asks again at the next cycle.

A start takes its permit only when its command is live (see Gates): for a zone, when its writes are live, `load_management_live` is on and its load is live. A dry-run start only checks the gate, so a decision that is only journaled never delays a real start; within one cycle, though, a would-be permit holds back the dry-run starts after it, as a real one would. The heating cycle writes a dry-run start as before and, when its permit would have been refused, journals `start_would_wait_dry_run` once per waiting episode: a load waits from the first cycle that would refuse its start until a cycle that does not, because the start would be granted or is not asked. The entry goes with the write at the zone's turn in the first cycle of the episode, and only when that write still starts the zone: a zone that a poll shows at its target, or heating, by its turn starts nothing and gets no entry. A dry-run check stays quiet, like a dry-run limp shed, until the meter has delivered a reading since teplo started. A zone without a load is never gated, and neither is a start before the guard has watched its first second.

### Phase meter lost

For the first 60 seconds a stale meter only stops new starts and restores. After 60 seconds stale the meter is lost and the house runs limp: at most one drawing floor per phase, floors of unknown phase counting as one more phase; the others are shed by priority, one a second, with the reason `meter_lost`, and no plug or heat source starts or comes back. Each view goes through the drawing floors from the most important down and keeps each whose phase no floor kept before it has. The live view counts every floor that draws, live or not, and leaves a floor that is not live drawing, as it cannot shed it, so it sheds a live floor only beside a more important one, and a floor that is not live yet never costs the most important floor its heat. A floor of unknown phase may heat beside a floor of its real phase, which limp mode cannot tell, so every floor's phase is set or learned before floors go live (`docs/deploy.md`). A floor starts again only while no other floor of its phase draws, 30 seconds apart like any start, so a dead meter never leaves the house without heat, and only where its phase allows it, as nothing may see the current its start adds: a phase that still reports needs room for the floor under the start ceiling, as with a fresh meter, and a phase that went silent while overloaded in the start's view refuses it with `phase_overloaded` until the phase reports again or the floor's `live` is unticked, as it holds a floor shed for that overload; a floor of unknown phase needs this of every phase. A start never knowingly pushes a silent phase past the shed threshold either: it needs the phase's last reading plus the floor's effective current at or below the shed threshold, 24 A by default, on every silent phase for a floor of unknown phase, and waits with `phase_would_overload` otherwise, until the phase reports again, while it stays the one floor allowed on the phase, a floor of unknown phase still counting as one more phase. The last reading is the measured current for a live start, and the measured current less the dry-run sheds still standing for a dry-run one, as for a phase that reports; it counts as it was, whatever has switched off since, and however long the phase has been silent, past the meter-lost time of the load settings included, as nothing newer is known. A phase that has not reported since teplo started has no last reading, and the start goes on the terms above. A shed floor comes back on the same terms: while the meter is lost, each view returns one of its shed floors a minute, the most important first, once no other floor of its phase draws and the phase it was shed for allows it. A phase that still reports, as when only another clamp has gone silent, allows it as a fresh meter would, once the phase has stayed at or below the restore threshold for 2 minutes in that view with room for the floor under the start ceiling, and the restore is journaled as `load_restored` with the cause `calm`. A silent phase allows it unless its overload in that view was still on when it went silent, and, for a floor shed for an overload, only if the phase was at or below the restore threshold at its last reading: a car that started charging after the overload ended, or a restart that forgot the overload, leaves no overload behind to hold the floor. The restore is journaled with the cause `limp`. A floor that its silent phase does not allow back stays off until the phase reports again and calms, or until its `live` is unticked: the load behind the overload, such as a charging car, may still be there, and nothing would see the overload come back. A restored floor's hold ends and the heating cycle switches its thermostat back on at its next allowed write, with a start permit, so floors shed by limp mode, or by an overload that ended before the meter went silent on a calm phase, do not stay off until it reports again. That write may come up to 30 minutes after the restore, and its permit checks the phase again as any start's does: a phase that overloaded again meanwhile and went silent keeps the floor off. A floor an overload shed also keeps the phase it was shed for from its live restore until a reading taken after the shed shows it heating, or it is shed again: while the meter is lost, its start needs that phase, when silent, to have been at or below the restore threshold at its last reading, as its restore did, and is refused with `phase_not_calm` otherwise, so a phase that rose without overloading after the restore and then went silent does not let the floor start blind. The guard keeps this hand-back with the load's live shed, the shed marked as handed back, so a restart resumes it as well; it ends, and is cleared from the load, once a reading taken after the shed, or after the restart, shows the floor heating, or once the floor is shed again. A dry-run restore hands nothing back. A shed plug stays off until the meter is fresh again and its phase has calmed, as no plug starts on a lost meter. Live loads run limp from teplo's start, as a meter that has not reported since then counts as stale from the start. A dry-run limp shed is journaled only after the meter has delivered a reading since teplo started: the device-lost alert already covers a meter that never reports (section 9), so a meter whose clamps are not installed yet stays quiet. The panel hatches the phase bars of a stale meter (section 10).

### Heat sources

A fast heat source teplo commands is a load of kind `source` (`Load.heat_source`), as the office convector is.

- **Start.** The source cycle asks for a permit for each write that switches a source on (section 6), an air conditioner in `heat` included, as the heating cycle does for a floor, and asks only once the guard serves the source's load live, as its gates say (section 6, see Fast sources). A refused live start writes nothing and the source waits, «Ждёт включения»; a dry-run start that would wait is journaled once per waiting episode as `start_would_wait_dry_run`. The gate keeps each load's waiting episode separately, so a cycle that does not ask for a load never ends that load's episode, and neither «Ждёт включения» nor the dry-run entry flickers while both cycles ask. While the meter is stale or lost, no source starts, with the reason `meter_stale`, as a plug.
- **Shed.** A shed switches the unit off at once through the source's write gate, overriding its interval, under the source's lock, and stamps the write; the stamp also clears `commanded_power` and ends a hold by hand (section 6), as does the shed's record on its load. The source cycle holds the source meanwhile (`shed`). A stamp the database refuses keeps the source shed until its interval after the shed, as for a floor. The shed is stored with the unit it switched off and `was_on`, as for a plug.
- **Restore.** A restore writes nothing: it hands the source back to the source cycle, whose next start asks for a permit. Nothing is restored while the meter is stale or lost, as limp mode counts and returns floors only.
- **Release.** Switching the load's `live`, `load_management_live` or the source's `live` off, or pointing the source at another unit, releases a live shed: the unit the shed switched off is switched back on at the source's next allowed write, as nobody else drives it then, and `load_restored` names the release with «снова включил обогреватель». The release switches the power only, so the unit comes back at the setpoint it had when it was shed, which may be one the owner set above `max_setpoint`: teplo no longer commands it, and `max_setpoint` bounds the setpoints teplo gives. An air conditioner comes back in `heat`, as its power-on is `heat`, whatever mode it had when it was shed, a `cool` the owner set included.
- **Owner override.** An owner who switches a shed source back on by hand wins, as with a plug: the first poll that reads it on drops the shed, journaled as `load_shed_overridden`.
- **Restart.** A live shed stored on a source's load less than its interval before a restart holds the source's gate for the rest of that interval, as for a zone (section 4).
- **Estimate.** On the estimated phase a source counts its effective current while it is on, nothing from a live shed until a poll after it reads it, and its effective current from a live permit until a poll after its command reads it (see Estimated phase).
- **Learning.** A source's switching casts no vote in phase map learning: its current comes from its rated power, and on a phase without a clamp no step would show.

### Estimated phase

A phase without a clamp is estimated from the loads on it. The load settings name it, `estimated_phase`, with the base current of the estimate, `estimated_base_amps`, 2.0 A by default, for what else draws on it; `sites/barsoom` names L2 (section 3), and both are edited in the admin only (section 11). Every second, while the meter delivers a fresh current of that phase, the guard puts the estimate in its place: the base current plus a part for each load whose effective phase is that phase, by the first rule that applies.

1. A load teplo shed live counts nothing from the shed until a poll after the shed reads it.
2. A load given a live start permit, the heating cycle's start of its floor, the source cycle's start of its heat source or the guard's restore of its plug, counts its effective current from the permit until a poll after its command reads it. Both cycles ask for their permits first and write afterwards, after a write stamp that may wait on a locked database, so a poll stored between the permit and the command still reads the load off: the start counts whatever the polls read until the cycle tells the guard that its command went out, failed or not, and then until a poll after that moment. A permit whose command never goes out, as when the write gate holds the write back, stops counting 60 seconds after it was granted. The guard switches a restored plug on right after its permit, and a plug or a floor it switches back on when it releases a shed counts from that moment the same way.
3. A load without a usable reading, as when its device is unavailable or its last command failed after its latest reading, counts its effective current.
4. A plug, which meters itself, counts the current it reads while it is on and nothing while it is off.
5. A floor or a heat source counts its effective current while it draws and nothing while it idles, the convector its rated 8.7 A whenever it is on.

The estimate is a lower bound. A load of unknown or another phase is left out, as the estimate never guesses where a load sits, and nothing sees a kettle on that phase. A clamp on the phase is the real answer, after which `estimated_phase` is cleared.

On that phase the estimate drives load management as the measured current drives it on the others: the overload and its calm, the sheds and restores of each view, and the start permits. Two rules differ.

- **Only loads of the phase are shed.** A shed for an overload of the estimated phase takes only the drawing loads of that phase, never one of unknown phase, as a load the estimate does not count would leave the estimate as it was and the overload would never end.
- **A live shed counts once.** A live shed relieves the estimated phase not at all, as the estimate counts its load as off from the shed on, and relief would count the drop twice; for the same reason a live start on that phase finds the room a live shed made at once. The dry-run view still takes off every dry-run shed until it is restored, as nothing really drops.

The estimate replaces the meter's reading only while that reading is fresh, so staleness, the meter-lost time and limp mode follow the meter as before, and a phase that goes silent keeps its last estimate as its last reading. Phase learning, EV detection and the phase overload alert never see the estimate: they read the meter's own current of the phase, which on an empty input stays at a fraction of an ampere, so the estimate, which counts an unavailable device at its full current, never raises an alert. Every `phase_overload` and `phase_calm` of that phase, and every shed, restore and `start_would_wait_dry_run` entry on it, carries `estimated`, and the panel's journal says «по оценке» (section 10).

### Phase map learning

Learning is passive, so it works in dry-run. When a poll shows that a load changed since its previous reading, as a plug switching or its current moving by 2 A or more, or a thermostat's relay turning between heating and idle, the guard waits 35 seconds, until every load has been read after the change, and looks in its 2 minutes of samples between the two readings for the strongest step: at each second, the mean of the next 3 seconds less the mean of the previous 3, on each phase. The step is a vote for its phase when it is 1.5 A or more, goes the way of the change, up for on, and the other phases moved by 0.5 A or less at that second. A window another load changed in, or one the samples do not cover without a gap of more than 3 seconds, casts no vote. Three votes for one phase and none against among the load's last five learn the phase, with the median step as the load's current: the clamp sees what the breaker sees, which on a stabilized phase is more than the load's own current. The learned phase and current are stored and journaled as `load_phase_learned` only when the phase changes or the current moves by half an ampere or more. A thermostat that stays at its floor limit produces no step and teaches nothing. A **calibration** action, which switches each load in turn while the house is quiet (about 10 minutes), comes later.

### EV detection

A rise of 10–22 A on one phase within 15 seconds is watched when the samples cover those seconds without a gap and no load switching on explains it; it grows while the charger ramps up, and held for 5 minutes it is journaled as `ev_charging_started` on that phase. `ev_charging_stopped` follows when the phase falls back to within 3 A of its level before the rise, or drops by a similar step, and no load seen switching off explains the drop. The guard decides 35 seconds after the drop shows, once every load has been read after it, so `ev_charging_stopped` is journaled, and the EV badge leaves the phase bar, about 35 seconds after the car stopped: the effective currents of the loads of that phase, or of unknown phase, that switched off around the drop, the guard's own sheds aside, are taken off the level before the rise, and the charge goes on unless the phase is still within 3 A of that lower level; a drop no such load explains ends the charge. So a floor that was heating when the charge began no longer ends the charge when its relay opens. The guard's own live sheds do not hide a charge, though the overload they answer is often the charge itself: the effective current of a live shed made within a rise is added back to the rise, the level a stop is measured against moves down and back up by the effective currents of the live sheds made and restored on the phase since the rise, a drop within 18 seconds of a live shed on the phase is not taken for a stop, and no rise is looked for while a live shed on the phase is younger than 5 seconds, as its drop may not show yet. EV detection only journals: a charging car reduces the headroom, which the guard already sees in the measured current. Recommended car setting at home: 16 A or less.

### Journal

Overloads, sheds and restores are written to the event journal immediately, so a trip can be traced after power returns; the overload entry is written before the shed it causes. Each entry names the load or the phase as its subject and keeps the raw values:

| Entry | Written when | Payload |
|---|---|---|
| `phase_overload` | a phase becomes overloaded | phase, current, `estimated` when the current is the estimate of a phase without a clamp (see Estimated phase) |

| `load_shed`, `load_shed_dry_run` | a load is shed, or would be; a plug whose shed write failed, once a reading shows it off or its load is pointed at another plug | load, name, kind, the phase it was shed for, that phase's current (none while the meter is lost), the load's effective current, the reason: `overload` or `meter_lost`, and `estimated` when that phase is estimated |

### Outages

- Every power cut is recorded, and the owner marks its cause in the panel: an overload that tripped our breaker, the village grid, or unknown (sections 9 and 10).
- Overload accounting and anything learned from trips count only outages marked overload. Grid, unknown and unmarked outages never count, so an outage of the village grid never reads as a trip.
- When `phase_overload` was journaled on a measured current within 2 minutes before an outage began, the panel puts the overload answer forward: the outage card says which phase was overloaded and marks the overload answer, and the outage's journal entry says the overload preceded the cut. The owner still confirms the cause; nothing is marked automatically. An overload by the estimate of a phase without a clamp is never put forward, as nothing measured it (see Estimated phase).

## 8. Tariffs

A tariff is a list of daily intervals, each `cheap`, `normal` or `expensive`; the default site has 23:00–07:00 cheap.
Uses: optimal start prefers to finish pre-heating within a cheap interval when comfort allows, and a water heater can be limited to cheap intervals. The tariff never overrides comfort.

## 9. Reliability

- **Clock.** The clock is trusted when NTP has synchronised since boot. Otherwise schedules pause (section 6) and the panel offers "take time from this device", which stores an offset inside teplo without touching the system clock. A last command stamped later than the clock (the clock moved back, e.g. after a reboot) does not hold back the next write, beyond the 30 minutes after a live shed, or after a guard write the heating cycle could not have read, that the write gate counts on the monotonic clock (section 4). Likewise a boost counts only while it ends within its 2-hour duration from now, trusted clock or not, so a boost stamped before the clock moved back cannot hold a zone at the boost setpoint, and so does a hold by hand (section 6). Each job that asks whether the clock is trusted reads the NTP status before it reads the time, so a time read just before NTP sets the clock is never taken as trusted.
- **Supervision.** An Entware init script starts teplo at boot; a cron job restarts it if the process is gone and lists each restart in `restarts.log`. Planned: an alert after more than 3 restarts an hour. The teplo resumed alert does not catch a restart loop, as each gap is shorter than its 3 minutes.
- **Device loss.** A device without a reading for 10 minutes appears in the panel as unavailable; one that stays unavailable, or a phase meter that stays stale, sends an alert (see Alerts below). A failed read or write keeps its error code (`device_unavailable`, `device_protocol_error`, `driver_not_implemented`, `device_not_found`, `secret_missing`, `device_config_invalid`, `plug_not_switchable` for a plug load management asks to switch through a driver that only reads, `climate_unit_not_controllable` for a climate unit teplo asks to command through a driver that only reads, and for the ESPHome devices `sensor_reading_failed` and `broker_unavailable`) beside the driver's own error text: the state store holds both, the log records both, and the journal entries `device_availability_changed`, `zone_command_failed` and `load_command_failed` store the code as `error_code` next to `reason` and `error`. A secrets table with an empty address or a protocol other than 3.1–3.5, or one tinytuya refuses, fails as `device_config_invalid` for that device alone: the poll records it and goes on to the next device.
- **Power loss.** The router has no UPS, so a power cut reboots it. At start teplo reads the router's uptime, the first number in `/proc/uptime`, and its boot id, the UUID in `/proc/sys/kernel/random/boot_id` that the kernel draws anew at every boot, and journals both in `teplo_started` beside the last activity, as `router_uptime` in seconds and `boot_id`; an unreadable or garbled boot id is journaled as null and logs one warning. Whether the router's current boot followed a power cut is read from these entries, not kept in memory. The start that opened the boot is found at start by reading the journaled starts back from the newest: it is the earliest of those carrying the current boot id before one that carries another boot id or none. The boot follows a power cut when the start before it journaled another boot id. When that start journaled none, as the last start before boot ids were journaled, or when the current boot id is unreadable and a start can judge only itself, the boot follows a power cut when the router came up less than 10 minutes before the start that opened it and after the last activity that start journaled; once the clock is trusted, the router came up at the current time less its uptime. A router rebooted for another reason, by hand or for a firmware update, reads as a power cut as well; a missing or garbled `/proc/uptime` counts as a restart and logs one warning. The outage of the boot runs from the last activity journaled by the start that opened it to the moment that start happened, which on the trusted clock is the moment the router came up plus the uptime that start journaled. Every start in the boot reads the same evidence, so a restart of teplo alone (a deploy, a crash, the watchdog) neither makes a second power cut nor loses the first, however long the router has been up and whether or not NTP had set the clock: a deploy that restarts teplo at 20:40, after it came back at 20:34 from a cut at 20:24 and recorded the cut at 20:35, finds the cut on record and records nothing, and one that restarts teplo before NTP synchronises, or in the minute between NTP setting the clock and the check that reports the cut, records the cut from 20:24 to 20:34 once the clock is trusted. Without a readable boot id a start can tell only its own outage: a restart before the power cut is stored still follows it, from the last activity before the boot to the restart, while the router has been up for less than 10 minutes and teplo has stamped no time on the set clock since the boot. The restart loses the power cut once the router has been up for 10 minutes, or once teplo has stamped a time on the set clock, as in the minute between NTP setting the clock and the check that stores the cut: that time falls after the boot, so the restart reads as one of teplo alone. The evidence covers the current boot only. Should the power go off again before the cut is stored, the outage of the next boot begins at the last activity before the second cut. Until teplo stamps a time on the set clock, that is still the last activity before the first cut, so the two cuts read as one outage spanning both (see Alerts below). A time stamped after NTP has set the clock, in the minute before the check stores the cut or while the database fails to store it, begins the outage instead, and the first cut is recorded nowhere: after a cut at 20:24, NTP at 20:35 and a command journaled at 20:35:05, a second cut at 20:35:45 records one outage from 20:35:05. A power cut is recorded whatever its length, once the clock is trusted (see Alerts below): the `power` context stores the outage with its cause unmarked and the boot id, and journals it as `power_outage` with the outage id and the interval at the moment it is stored, in one transaction, so a failure of either write keeps neither and an outage never lacks the journal entry where its cause can be changed later; the panel asks for the cause (section 10). The boot id is unique among outages, so the outage of a boot is stored once, whichever start in the boot stores it; outages recorded before boot ids and those entered in the admin carry none. The admin does not delete an outage that carries a boot id, as the starts of its boot stay in the journal and the next start in that boot would record the power cut again and send its notice anew; its cause can be changed instead, and an outage entered by hand can still be deleted. Should the database fail to store them, e.g. while it stays locked, the check logs `outage_record_failed` with the interval and tries again every minute until it succeeds, and a restart meanwhile finds the power cut in the journal again as long as the boot id is readable. On start teplo also reads every device and reconciles the desired state. Thermostats may come back switched off and the zone's next allowed write switches them on. Every Tuya driver takes its client from one shared function that disables tinytuya's device22 fallback, so the client keeps the full status query and a device that answers badly while booting is read normally afterwards.
- **Flash wear.** History is written in batches every 5 minutes; journal events are written immediately. Pruning history to one year of minute samples is planned; until then it only grows, in the reference house by 54 rows a minute (three for each of the five thermostats, four for each of the five metered plugs, two for each of the four air sensors, one for the outdoor sensor, three for the convector, one for the office computer while it reports and six for the phase meter), about 28 million rows a year. The start takes the latest time among the newest 5000 samples by row id, about an hour and a half of history in the reference house, rather than scanning the whole table, so it does not slow down as history grows. It also takes the last activity the previous start recorded, so the last time taken on a set clock carries across starts that come before NTP synchronises, until the clock, still running behind, stamps a later time (see Alerts below).
- **Coexistence.** teplo runs at nice 10 on its own ports (panel 8088, MQTT 1883); the watchdog restarts it when its resident memory exceeds 300 MB.

### Alerts

The `notifications` context sends alerts to one Telegram chat. A pure policy decides when an alert is raised and cleared; each raise and clear is journaled as `alert_raised` or `alert_cleared`, which the panel's journal shows in the page's language, and queued for Telegram.

| Alert | Raised when | Cleared when |
|---|---|---|
| Device lost | a thermostat, plug, temperature sensor or climate unit has stayed unavailable for 2 minutes, about 12 minutes after its last reading; the phase meter has stayed stale for 1 minute (section 7), one to two minutes after its last current; a device without a reading since teplo started, or a phase meter without a current of every phase, 10 minutes after the start | the device reads again; the phase meter is no longer stale |
| Zone off | the zone's floor target wants the thermostat on (any heating-enabled zone, day or night, live or dry-run) and the thermostat has reported power off for 35 minutes, not counting a hold by hand | the thermostat reports power on, or the zone no longer wants it on |
| Phase overload | a phase's measured current has stayed above the shed threshold for 1 minute (section 7), e.g. «Фаза L2 перегружена: 31 А» | the phase has stayed at or below the restore threshold for 2 minutes: «Фаза L2 снова в норме» |
| teplo resumed | teplo starts after a power cut, once per power cut and at any gap from its last activity, or after a restart of teplo alone more than 3 minutes after its last activity | — |

- A job checks the alerts every minute. An alert is sent once and its recovery once. The state lives in memory, so a restart forgets which alerts were raised: a device that is still lost alerts again 10 minutes later and a zone that is still off 35 minutes later, while one that recovers before that sends no recovery. For example, «Кухня: нет связи с термостатом с 20:24» goes out, a deploy restarts teplo at 20:40, the thermostat answers at 20:45, and no «снова на связи» follows; the panel and the journal show the thermostat back. Restoring raised alerts from the journal would instead send recoveries of alerts that never reached the chat, as the journal does not record delivery.
- Durations are measured on the monotonic clock, so a clock step after a reboot neither raises nor hides an alert; a reading taken before the step keeps its true age, so a device that goes silent right after NTP sets the clock alerts about 12 minutes after its last reading, as any other.
- The moment a message names is kept on the monotonic clock as well: when teplo first notices the condition it records the last reading's age, or the notice itself for a device that has not answered since the start and for a zone that is off. The moment becomes wall-clock time only when the alert is raised, so a clock set in between does not shift it; an alert raised while the clock is untrusted names no time, e.g. «Кухня: нет связи с термостатом», and is not held back. The text itself is written when the message is sent, and a moment on an earlier day than the sending names its date: an alert raised on 1 November that waits out a Telegram outage until 3 November reads «Бойлер 1 этаж: нет связи с розеткой с 1 ноября 21:15», where one sent the same evening reads «… с 21:15».
- The monitored devices come from a list of sources: the thermostats, named by their zone, and the metered plugs, the temperature sensors, the phase meter and the climate units, named by their device names. Presence sources are in none of them: one that goes silent only leaves its room's occupancy unknown (section 4). The text names the kind of device that went silent, e.g. «Кухня: нет связи с термостатом с 20:24», «Бойлер 1 этаж: нет связи с розеткой с 20:24» and «Воздух: кухня: нет связи с датчиком с 20:24», and its recovery «Бойлер 1 этаж: розетка снова на связи» or «Воздух: кухня: датчик снова на связи». The phase meter's own name says what it is, so its texts name no kind: «Фазометр: нет связи с 20:24» and «Фазометр: снова на связи»; so does a climate unit's, «Конвектор: кабинет: нет связи с 20:24».
- The phase meter counts as lost while it is stale (section 4), so a phase that stops reporting alerts as well as a silent node, and its message names the last current of the phase silent longest. The delay after teplo notices a loss is set per device kind in the alert policy: 1 minute for the phase meter, which load management cannot do without, and 2 minutes for the other kinds.
- Phase overload takes its holds from the load guard, which follows each phase every second on the measured current, so dry-run sheds, which relieve a phase only in the guard's books, never delay or clear it. On a phase without a clamp it follows the meter's own reading of the empty input, never the estimate load management works with (section 7), so an estimate never raises it. One alert covers an overload episode and names the phase's current at the end of the minute that raised it, to a tenth of an ampere; the alert job raises or clears it at its next check, within a minute of the guard. The monitored phases are a source of their own, named `L1` to `L3` as on the phase bars.
- Zone off takes the floor target from the room intent and the floor-mode decision the heating cycle uses (`RoomIntentReader`, `decide_floor_mode`), on the comfort windows the zone takes from its room. Guests and overrides never switch a floor off, so they neither raise nor clear it. 35 minutes exceed the 30-minute write budget, so a correction teplo is about to make never alerts. While the thermostat is unavailable its power is unknown and the zone-off timer stops; it starts over once the power is known again, as teplo can correct the thermostat only after it answers. The message still names the moment teplo first saw the thermostat off. A zone held by hand (section 6, Manual changes) is off by the owner's choice, so its timer stops the same way and starts over when the hold ends, which still gives teplo its 30 minutes to correct the thermostat, and an alert already raised sends no recovery while the hold lasts.
- teplo resumed: a job of its own checks every minute, once the clock is trusted, until the outages before the start are reported. The outage runs from the last journal entry or flushed sample to the start, in local time, e.g. «teplo снова работает. Перерыв 20:24–20:33 (9 мин)», and for a power cut to the start that opened the boot (see Power loss above). Like the alerts, the notice is dated against its sending: an outage from 23:10 to 23:40 whose notice goes out after midnight reads «teplo снова работает. Перерыв 31 октября 23:10–23:40 (30 мин)». After a power cut the notice gets a second line, «Отметь причину в панели: http://192.0.2.1:8088/», with the panel address from the `TEPLO_PANEL_URL` setting, or «Отметь причину в панели.» when the setting is empty; the notice after a restart of teplo alone stays one line. A power cut is reported at any gap, so a cut that teplo comes back from within 3 minutes of its last activity is reported and recorded too. Its notice goes out once its outage is stored, so a power cut sends one notice, whichever start in the boot stores it, and a start that finds the outage on record sends none; while the database fails to store it, the notice waits with it. A restart in a boot that followed a power cut is part of that power cut when its last activity falls before the start that opened the boot; when teplo was active after that start, the restart reports its own gap as any restart does, after the notice of the power cut if that is still to go. A deploy restart flushes the samples on stop and takes under a minute; after a power loss the outage may start up to 5 minutes early, one sample flush period, and if no device was readable and nothing was journaled for a while, it reaches back to the last record. The notice waits for a trusted clock and dates the start by teplo's uptime, and the start that opened the boot by the router's; should the start still fall before the last activity, as when a clock that ran ahead stamped the last activity, no outage can be measured from it, and the notice is held back instead of being dropped. The start stays the same at every check, so such a notice goes out only if a later clock step moves the start past the last activity. A restart is not journaled again, as `teplo_started` already holds the last activity; a power cut is journaled as `power_outage` (see Power loss above). After a boot the router's clock runs hours behind until NTP sets it, and the samples flushed meanwhile carry those early times, so the outage starts at the latest time among the newest samples, not at the time of the newest one: a second power cut before NTP synchronised is measured from the last sample taken on the set clock, and two cuts 13 minutes apart read as one outage spanning both, not as «Перерыв 15:32–20:40 (5 ч 8 мин)». The newest samples reach back only about an hour and a half, so the start also takes the last activity the previous start recorded: the last time taken on a set clock carries across starts that come before NTP synchronises, until the clock, still running behind, stamps a later time. A clock 5 hours behind does so after about 5 hours without internet, and a power cut after that is measured from the last time it stamped, which reads 5 hours before the cut.
- Texts are translated with gettext into `TEPLO_ALERT_LANGUAGE`, Russian by default.
- Delivery is best effort and never holds up control: a second job sends queued messages every minute, oldest first, through the Bot API `sendMessage` over HTTPS with the standard library, with a 10-second timeout and the optional HTTP proxy from the secrets file. A failed send stays queued and is retried. A raised alert waits as long as it lasts, so an outage of Telegram, the internet or the proxy delays it but never silences it; once the alert clears, it and its recovery wait one more hour and are dropped together, so a recovery never arrives without its alert. A recovery whose alert already went out and the teplo resumed notice wait an hour. The queue lives in memory, so a message still waiting when teplo stops is lost. That holds for the notice of a power cut too: the next start finds the outage on record and does not send it again, and the dashboard keeps asking for the cause until it is marked (section 10). A message Telegram refuses (4xx other than 429) is dropped and logged, and a refused alert takes its recovery with it, queued or still to come, so the chat never gets «Кухня: термостат снова на связи» without the alert before it. Without a `[telegram]` table alerts are only journaled, and the log says so once.
- `manage.py send_test_alert` sends a test message at once and prints `ok`, or fails with the error code and the reason, so a wrong token or chat, missing CA certificates or a blocked network shows up when the table is set rather than at the next outage. `docs/deploy.md` lists the codes.

### Commands

With `commands = true` in the `[telegram]` table the chat that receives the alerts also commands teplo. teplo reads the switch at start; without it nothing polls Telegram, and the log says `owner_commands_disabled` once.

- **Receiving.** The command listener long-polls the bot's `getUpdates` for messages, 10 seconds at a time with a 20-second HTTP timeout, through the alerts' proxy. A failed poll, with Telegram, the internet or the proxy out of reach or a second poller of the bot (below), is retried 30 seconds later; the log says `owner_commands_poll_failed` with the reason once per streak of failures, a journal that cannot be read to resume (below) included, and `owner_commands_poll_recovered` when a poll works again. A failed poll comes back from its use case as an outcome rather than an error, so nothing else is logged for it, and a day without internet writes two lines to the flash, not one every 30 seconds.
- **Which messages count.** Only text from the configured chat. Edited messages and messages sent more than 10 minutes before teplo reads them are dropped, so an outage does not replay old commands. Their age is judged on a trusted clock only: while the clock is untrusted, as after a power cut until NTP sets a clock that runs hours behind, the listener does not poll, logs `owner_commands_wait_for_clock` once and looks at the clock again every 30 seconds, and Telegram keeps the messages meanwhile, so the first poll on the set clock drops those sent while the router was off.
- **Journaled before it runs.** Each command is journaled as `owner_command` with its update id, the bot's id, which is the public numeric part of its token, the command it was read as and the first 200 characters of its text, before it runs. Telegram confirms an update only at the next poll, so at start teplo resumes after the last update its bot journaled: a restart between running a command and that poll never runs it twice. A command the journal cannot take is answered «База данных занята: повторите через минуту.» and not run, and while the journal cannot be read at start teplo does not poll. The same answer comes when the database refuses the command's change or a read before it, so it always means that nothing changed; once the change is stored, a reply that cannot read the floors back names the change alone, «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: 23,0° до 18:40.», or after «авто» «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: снова по расписанию.», and «Кабинет: снова по присутствию.» for a room that returns to occupancy, and a repeated «холодно» never steps the target twice by mistake.
- **One answer.** Each command gets one reply at once through `sendMessage`, outside the alert queue, which sends once a minute; a reply Telegram does not take is logged as `owner_reply_failed` and not sent again. Replies are written in `TEPLO_ALERT_LANGUAGE`.
- **Commands.** Commands run the panel's use cases with the source `telegram` and never write to a device: like the panel's −/+ and boost, what they change reaches a floor through its gates at its next allowed write (section 6).
  - «гости» switches guests on for the coming night, «гости 2» and «гости 3» (or «гости 2 ночи») for two and three nights, and «гости всё» (or «стоп», «нет», «уехали», «выкл») ends them early (see Room intent, section 6). Each is read only as the whole message, so a sentence that starts with «гости», as «гости все здесь» or «гости 2 человека», gets the list of commands and changes nothing. A house without a guest room says so.
  - «холодно в <комната>» sets the room's target 1 °C warmer for 2 hours, and at least to its comfort target; «жарко в <комната>» 1 °C cooler; «<комната> 22» sets 22 °C, «22,5» with a comma or a point, within 10–30 °C; «<комната> авто» clears the override and names the target of the schedule, or in a room that follows occupancy the target occupancy gives, «Кабинет: снова по присутствию, 19,0°.». They act on an adjustable room, the office included, as its convector heats it: «холодно в офисе» sets the office's comfort, 21.5 °C, for 2 hours when it holds its setback of 19.0 °C. In a room whose floors read no air, as a bathroom, «холодно» boosts its floors, and «жарко» and a target are answered that only «буст» works there; a room teplo has nothing to heat in, as the bedroom, says so.
  - «буст <комната>» boosts every floor of the room for 2 hours, as the boost of the panel does, in one transaction, as «холодно» does in a room whose floors read no air: when the database refuses one floor, no floor is boosted and no write budget freed, and the reply is the busy answer, so a repeated «буст» frees each floor's budget once.
  - «топлю камин» (or «разжёг камин», in either order) marks every fireplace of the house lit for 4 hours and names the end, «Камин топится до 22:10.»; «камин погас» (or «потух», «прогорел») ends the mark, «Камин погас.», or says «Камин и так не отмечен.» when no mark counted. Each is read only as a message of exactly these two words (see External heat, section 6); a house without a fireplace says «В доме нет камина.»
  - «статус» lists the guests, each room with a floor, an air sensor or a heat source teplo commands with its air, its target and why it holds it, and the occupancy of a room with presence sources, «Кабинет: цель 21,5°, комфорт до 23:45; никого (наблюдение)» while the room only watches it or «Кабинет: цель 19,0°, никого» once it follows it, each floor's reading and setpoint, what each source reads, «Конвектор: кабинет: включён на 22°, у прибора 21°» or «… включён на 22°» without its own temperature, «Кондиционер: спальня: охлаждение, 24°, teplo не трогает» for a unit the owner cools and «… авто, 24°» or «… другой режим, 24°» for one teplo takes back to heat, with «…; блокировка морозом, на улице -17°» or «…; блокировка: нет показаний с улицы» while it is locked out, and the phases, a phase without a clamp by its estimate (section 7), «L2 ≈8,4 А».
  - Anything else gets the list of commands.
- **Rooms by name.** A room is named by the word stems of `Room.aliases`, which the site data sets and the admin edits, «гостин кухн прихож зал» for the open space, or else by its whole name; a floor's number or ordinal, «санузел 2» or «на втором этаже», picks between rooms of the same stems, as the two bathrooms. A name that fits several rooms is asked back, «Какая комната: Санузел 1 этаж или Санузел 2 этаж?», and one that fits none gets the list of rooms.
- **Replies.** A reply names the target and its end and each floor's setpoint with the earliest moment its write budget allows the write, or says that load management holds the floor off or that it is held by hand: «холодно в гостиной» reads «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: 23,0° до 18:40.» with «Гостиная: пол 30° в ближайшую минуту», «Кухня: пол 30° не раньше 16:52», «Прихожая: пол отключён управлением нагрузкой» or «Прихожая: изменён на термостате, teplo не трогает до 18:40». A heat source of the room gets a line of its own the same way, the setpoint its unit takes and when its own write gate lets it through: «холодно в офисе» reads «Кабинет: 21,5° до 18:40.» with «Конвектор: кабинет: 22° в ближайшую минуту», «… 22° не раньше 16:52», «… 22° — наблюдение, команда не уходит», «… выключен управлением нагрузкой», «… изменён на приборе, teplo не трогает до 18:40», «… уже 22°» or «… нет связи».
- **One poller per bot.** Telegram answers a second poller of the same bot with 409 Conflict, both of them, so one teplo at most runs with `commands = true` per bot.

## 10. Panel

Server-rendered Django templates with HTMX; the floors with their rooms, the house facts above them and the loads block refresh every 10 seconds (thermostats and plugs are polled every 30 seconds, the air sensors report every 30 seconds) and the phase bars every 3 seconds from a partial of their own; all assets are served locally.

- **Dashboard:** a section per floor with a card per room: its zones as floor tiles (air, floor, target and boost), the target of an adjustable room with −/+, the air of a room without a zone in large type with no floor band, and its plugs. A room without a zone, an air sensor, a plug, a heat source, a fireplace or a source teplo commands, or a presence source is left out, and the zones and plugs in no room follow under «Без комнаты», or without that heading in a house that has no floors. Then the shedding order, where each load shows its state (drawing, idle, shed, waiting to start), and phase bars to 25 A with the threshold marks and the EV badge; above the floors, outdoor temperature, clock and device warnings, and a question about each power cut whose cause is not marked.
- **Schedule:** every room's comfort windows per weekday with the night, warm-up and comfort periods they make, and when each of its zones starts warming up; windows are added, changed, deleted and copied (see Schedule and settings below).
- **Settings:** per room whether it is a guest room, its comfort and night air targets and its backup heat, followed by its zones; per zone its name, heating, floor setpoints, air targets outside a room climate and pre-heat lead; the climate settings with the hour guests leave; the load settings and the shedding order with each load's phase and nominal current; the gates read-only. Tariffs, the water heater cheap-only switch and calibration come later.
- **History:** temperatures, phase currents, zone states; the event journal, where the cause of a power cut can be changed.

A JSON API under `api/v1` exposes the same state: `zones/`, `rooms/`, `sources/`, `plugs/`, `sensors/`, `presence/`, `phases/` and the read-only `loads/` and `outages/`. `zones/` gives each zone with its readings, its floor target and its gates, `live_control` and `air_control_live`, the path that drives it, `path`: `floor`, `air` or `air_dry_run`, with `air_target`, the air target on the air paths and null in floor mode, the slug of its `room`, null for a zone in no room, and `manual_until`, the end of a hold by hand while one applies and null otherwise (section 6). `rooms/` lists every room in floor order, those the dashboard leaves out included, with its `slug`, `name`, the slug of its `floor`, the slugs of its `zones`, the sorted slugs of the `devices` placed in it, its `comfort_target` and `setback_target`, its `air_sensor`, its `backup_zone`, `backup_live` and whether backup heat is on, `backup_active`, the `reason` (`override`, `occupied`, `vacant`, `clock`, `guests`, `comfort` or `setback`), `target` and `until` of the target it holds now (see Room intent, section 6), whether it is a `guest_room` and `adjustable`, its `occupancy`, `occupied`, `vacant` or `unknown` and null for a room without presence sources, and whether its target follows it, `follows_occupancy`; a room without a room climate gives null targets, sensor, zone, reason, target and end and false for the rest. `sources/` lists every heat source in floor and room order, with its `slug`, `name`, the slug of its `room`, its `kind`, the slug of its `device` (null for a fireplace), its `priority` and its gate `live`, what its unit reads, `available`, `on`, its `mode` (`heat`, `cool`, `auto`, `other` or `off`, null for a fireplace and a unit never read), `setpoint`, `level` and `temperature`, the `target_setpoint` teplo would give it, whether the outdoor lockout holds it, `locked_out`, the end of a hold by hand, `manual_until`, whether load management holds it `shed`, and for a fireplace its mark, `lit_until`, and whether the detector sees a fire, `burning`; what a source does not have reads null or false. `sensors/` lists every temperature sensor by name with its slug, kind, temperature, humidity, availability and the age of its reading in whole seconds, `age_seconds`; a sensor keeps its last reading once it is unavailable, as the store does. `presence/` lists every presence source by name with its `slug`, `name`, the slug of its `room`, the `node` it is heard on, `teplo-pc-office`, whether it is `available`, whether its latest report saw someone, `present`, the age of that report and of the last report of someone in whole seconds, `age_seconds` and `seen_age_seconds`, and the `error_code` of its failure; it shows whether the office computer reports. `phases/` lists `l1`, `l2` and `l3` with the latest current of each in amperes, whether it is stale and its `age_seconds`, `ev_amps`, the step of an EV charging on that phase or null, and `estimated`, true when `amps` is the estimate of a phase without a clamp (section 7), and is empty without a phase meter. `loads/` lists the loads in shedding order with their `slug`, `name`, `kind`, the `zone` or the `device` they control, `priority`, the effective `phase` and its `phase_origin` (`set`, `learned` or `unknown`), the `learned_phase`, `learned_amps` and `learning_samples` of passive learning, `nominal_amps`, the effective `amps` and its `amps_origin` (`nominal`, `learned`, `rated` or `default`), the `state` (`drawing`, `idle`, `shed`, `shed_dry_run`, `waiting`, `waiting_dry_run` or `unavailable`) and `live`, whether its commands reach its device. `outages/` lists every recorded power cut newest first with its `id`, `began_at`, `ended_at`, `cause` and `cause_marked_at`. Access requires the panel password: a single Django user `owner` is created from the secrets file, and the session is remembered per device. Devices, sensors, floors and rooms, the rooms' climates with their air sensor, backup zone and comfort windows, their gate `follows_occupancy`, which their list edits too and which the admin refuses, on the page and in the list alike, for a room with a zone, and their `vacant_after_minutes`, the heat sources (`/admin/climate/heatsource/`: name, room, kind, device, priority, the gate `live` editable in the list and the fireplace's mark read-only, where a fireplace has no device and any other kind needs one, as the database keeps it; the page of a heater or an air conditioner edits its `setpoint_bias`, `max_setpoint`, `min_outdoor`, `min_write_minutes` and `rated_watts` and shows what teplo stored, its stamps, its hold and what it last commanded, read-only; the page refuses a heater or an air conditioner in a room with a floor or with a climate unit whose driver only reads, «Обогревателю и кондиционеру нужен прибор, которым драйвер умеет управлять, например tuya_heater или esphome_climate», and an air conditioner's `max_setpoint` below 17.0 °C, «Источник этого вида не принимает уставку ниже 17°, поэтому max setpoint не может быть ниже»; saving it places its device in its room, whose page then shows the room read-only; the list's column «Команды» names the gates that hold a source's commands back, `TEPLO_DRY_RUN`, the source's `live`, `load management live`, a missing load or the load's `live`), the load settings' estimated phase and the base current of its estimate (section 7), and the gates are edited in the Django admin. A device's page sets its room and, for a device heard on another device's ESPHome node, its `node`, which the list of devices shows; saving a zone's page moves the zone's thermostat and air sensor into the zone's room, and their own pages show the room read-only. A room climate refuses an air sensor that serves a zone or is placed in another room; the site data creates the room climates, so the admin neither adds nor deletes one. A room climate's page edits whether the room is a guest room and shows its override read-only, and the climate settings' page edits the hour guests leave and shows their end read-only, as the panel sets both; it also switches the manual hold, `manual_hold_live`, which the list of climate settings edits too, while a zone's page shows the end of its hold read-only (section 6). A zone's page edits the zone's own comfort windows only while its room holds no comfort. The zone learning list stays read-only and has one action, «Сбросить обучение», which resets the zones selected and names them, «Обучение сброшено: Гостиная, Кухня» (section 6).

The visual language is a thermal camera. An ironbow ramp maps 14 to 32 °C and is the only saturated colour, so colour always means floor temperature. Each zone is a floor tile in its room's card: its reading is set in large type, and its floor band glows in the ramp colour at the point of the scale where the floor reads, with the target marked on the same scale. Dark and light themes follow the device setting. Fonts (Unbounded for readings, Golos Text for text, both OFL) and the Lucide icons (ISC) are vendored with their licenses. The interface is English and Russian, picked from the browser's language; floors are listed by level, the rooms of a floor by their position and then by name, and the zones, air sensors and plugs of a room by name.

A zone with an air sensor shows the air temperature beside the floor reading, in large type with the humidity small, and in ink, as colour means floor temperature. A lost air sensor is hatched and says what to check, like a lost plug, while the floor reading stays. The house facts above the floors start with the outdoor temperature from the climate settings' outdoor sensor; in a house with a guest room they also switch guests: «Гости на» «1 ночь», «2 ночи» or «3 ночи», titled with the guest rooms, switches them on for that many nights, the coming one first, as «гости», «гости 2» and «гости 3» do in the chat. While they stay, a chip «Гости до 14:00 завтра» stands before the same buttons, which set the guests' end anew from now and so keep them longer, and «Гости уехали» ends them early. A row below the facts lists the other air sensors that belong to no room and serve no zone, with their temperature and humidity. A room without a zone of its own, as the bedroom, shows its air sensor on a tile of its own: the air in large type with the humidity small, captioned «Воздух», in ink and without the floor band, as colour means floor temperature; a lost sensor is hatched and says what to check. A room with zones lists any other air sensor placed in it in a row under its floor tiles.

A zone with an air sensor also names the path that drives it (section 6) in a chip beside its schedule state: «По полу» for floor mode, «По воздуху» for air mode, and «По воздуху: наблюдение» for air mode in dry-run, where floor mode drives the thermostat and air mode is only journaled. The chips are drawn in ink, the one for air mode filled. On the air paths the air caption gives the air target, «Воздух, цель 22,0°», as the floor caption gives the floor target; in air mode the floor target is the floor limit while the room heats and the idle floor setpoint while it waits. A zone in pre-heat follows the start of comfort with the lead of its pre-heat, «Комфорт с 06:30. Разогрев начат за 2 ч 40 мин»: the held optimal lead in air mode, the fixed lead on the other paths. At night a zone in air mode dates its next warm-up by the optimal lead of the minute, which moves as the room cools. The panel decides each zone as the heating cycle does, from the readings, what the zone has learned and the cycle's control book, so a card shows the mode, the target and the held pre-heat start the cycle works with, the raise of live backup heat included. A room with backup heat says so beside its name: «Резерв: Санузел 2 этаж» while backup heat is on, with «Наблюдение» while its gate is off or teplo only watches, and «Резерв ниже 19,0°: Санузел 2 этаж» while it is off.

A room with a fireplace shows a row for it on its card: while a mark counts, a chip «Камин топится до 22:10» and a quiet button «Камин погас», otherwise a button «Топлю камин»; both post to `rooms/<slug>/fireplace/`, which marks every fireplace of the room and refreshes the floors. While the detector sees a fire in the room, a chip says «Похоже, топится камин», with «Наблюдение» beside it while the fireplace's `live` is off and the detector only journals (section 6). A room's target follows its intent (section 6). An adjustable room shows it between two buttons, «Цель 22,0°» with − and +, which step it by 0.5 °C for 2 hours and are disabled where a step would leave 10–30 °C. A room with presence sources shows its occupancy in a chip beside its name, in ink, as colour means floor temperature: «Занято» filled, «Никого», or «Нет данных» dashed, titled with what each source saw, «Компьютер: кабинет: кто-то был 3 мин назад · Радар: кабинет: нет связи», and followed by «Наблюдение» while the room's `follows_occupancy` is off. The sheds and the waiting starts come from the guard's book in memory, so they appear within 10 seconds and a restart clears the dry-run ones. A load whose commands do not reach its device, because `TEPLO_DRY_RUN`, `load_management_live`, its own `live`, for a zone `live_control` or for a heat source the source's `live` holds them back, carries the «Наблюдение» chip, as a zone does.

Below the loads, the phase bars show the current of L1, L2 and L3 against a scale to 25 A with the value beside each bar, and tick marks at the start ceiling and the shed threshold of the load settings, 20 A and 24 A by default (section 7); a shed threshold set above 24 A stretches the scale to 1 A past it. A phase on which EV detection sees a car charging carries a badge under its bar with the step of the charge, «Электромобиль: 16 А», for as long as the guard keeps the charge. They are drawn in ink only. A stale meter is hatched and says what to check, and a phase without a current from the last 10 seconds shows neither bar nor value; without a phase meter the block is left out. A phase without a clamp shows the estimate load management works with (section 7) while the meter reports the phase: its bar is drawn as a dashed outline, never hatched, as hatching means a stale meter, its value reads «≈ 14,4 А» with «≈ оценка» under the bar, and the row's title lists the parts of the estimate, «база 2,0 А · Бойлер 2 этаж 6,4 А · Санузел 1 этаж 6,0 А».

Above the floors, a card asks about each power cut whose cause is not marked, newest first: «Свет пропадал 20:24–20:33 (9 мин). Что это было?» with three answers, «Перегруз: выбило наш автомат», «Пропало в посёлке» and «Не знаю». The interval is dated like the alerts: the date appears when it is not today in local time, e.g. «Свет пропадал 31 октября 20:24–20:33 (9 мин)». An answer is stored with the time it was given and the card leaves the dashboard; the cards refresh with the zone cards, so a power cut recorded while the page is open appears within 10 seconds. The journal shows each `power_outage` entry with its interval, its current cause and the same three answers, so a cause can be changed later. When `phase_overload` was journaled within 2 minutes before the cut began (section 7), the card says so below its question, «Перед отключением фаза L2 была перегружена: 26,4 А», and puts the overload answer forward: it stays first and is drawn heavier with a mark, but no cause is stored until the owner answers. The journal entry of that cut carries the same line, and its answers mark the overload answer the same way while the cause is unmarked. Like the loads block, the cards use no ramp colour.

The journal words the dry-run entries of section 6, in ink: «Кухня: в режиме по воздуху включил бы обогрев (20,9° при цели 22,0°)» or «… остановил бы обогрев …», with the setpoint air mode would give the thermostat below it, «На термостате было бы 30°»; an entry with no would-be state before it, as the first after teplo starts, after the zone's air sensor returns or after its air control is switched off, reads as an observation resumed rather than a change, «Кухня: наблюдение по воздуху возобновлено, сейчас грел бы (20,9° при цели 22,0°)» or «… сейчас не грел бы …», with the same note below it; an entry whose would-be state is the one the zone already followed changes only the thermostat, as when a boost starts or ends or heating is switched off, and reads «Кухня: в режиме по воздуху держал бы термостат на 30°» or «… выключил бы термостат», without a note; «Кухня: в режиме по воздуху начал бы разогрев в 04:10, а не в 03:30», or «… тоже начал бы разогрев в 03:30» when the two starts agree; and «Санузел 2 этаж: резервный обогрев включил бы комфорт (Спальня 18,8°)» or «… вернул бы расписание (Спальня 19,3°)», while entries of the second-floor guard of earlier releases still read «Санузел 2 этаж: защита второго этажа включила бы комфорт (2 этаж 18,8°)». The other values, such as the schedule mode, the start and early-stop offsets in effect and the lead, stay in the payload.

The journal words the entries of load management (section 7) the same way, naming the load, or the phase as L1 to L3: «Перегрузка фазы L2: 26,4 А» and «Перегрузка фазы L2 прошла: 17,2 А»; a shed, «Бойлер 1 этаж: выключил розетку» or «Кухня: выключил термостат», with its reason below, «Перегрузка фазы L2: 26,4 А» or «Нет данных от фазометра: по одному полу на фазу»; a restore, «Бойлер 1 этаж: снова включил розетку» or «Кухня: вернул термостат расписанию», with «Фаза L2 в норме: 17,2 А», the current of the view that decided the restore, «Фазы в норме» when the shed had no measured phase, «Нет данных от фазометра: на этой фазе не греет другой пол» for a floor back while the phase it was shed for is silent, or «Управление нагрузкой переключено в настройках» for a release, where a zone whose `live_control` is off reads «Кухня: снова включил термостат», as teplo switched its thermostat on rather than handing it to the heating cycle; the dry-run twins in the conditional, «Бойлер 1 этаж: выключил бы розетку»; «Бойлер 1 этаж: розетку включили вручную, отключение снято»; a failed command, «Бойлер 1 этаж: не удалось выключить розетку» or «Кухня: не удалось снова включить термостат», explained by its error code like a zone command, such as «Драйвер этой розетки не умеет её переключать» for `plug_not_switchable`; «Кухня: отложил бы включение» with the reason the start would wait, such as «Фаза L2 дошла бы до 21,5 А», «Фазометр молчит, а фаза L2 по последнему показанию дошла бы до 24,5 А» or «Другая нагрузка только что включилась»; «Бойлер 1 этаж: выучил фазу L2, 6,2 А»; and «Фаза L3: похоже, заряжается электромобиль, +16,0 А» and «Фаза L3: электромобиль больше не заряжается». The phase overload alert reads as its message does, «Фаза L2 перегружена: 31,3 А». An entry on an estimated phase says so: «Перегрузка фазы L2 по оценке: 24,6 А» and «Перегрузка фазы L2 по оценке прошла: 17,2 А», a shed «Санузел 1 этаж: выключил бы термостат» with «Перегрузка фазы L2 по оценке: 24,6 А», a restore with «Фаза L2 по оценке в норме: 17,2 А», and a waiting start with «Фаза L2 по оценке дошла бы до 21,5 А» or «Фазометр молчит, а фаза L2 по последней оценке дошла бы до 24,5 А». A heat source's entries name the heater: «Конвектор: кабинет: выключил обогреватель», «… вернул обогреватель комнате» for a restore, «… снова включил обогреватель» for a release, «… не удалось выключить обогреватель», «… обогреватель включили вручную, отключение снято», and the conditional twins in dry-run.

The journal words the owner's intents too, naming a room by the name its entry carries: «Гости до 14:00 завтра» (`guests_set`, with the nights and where the command came from), «Режим гостей выключен» (`guests_cleared`, journaled only when guests were staying), «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: вручную 22,5° до 18:40» (`room_overridden`, with the room as its subject) and «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: снова по расписанию» (`room_override_cleared`, journaled only when an override was stored), or «Кабинет: снова по присутствию» when the room returns to occupancy; an intent that came from Telegram adds «Из Telegram» below. A room's occupancy reads «Кабинет: занято», «Кабинет: никого» or «Кабинет: нет данных о присутствии» (`room_occupancy_changed`), with «Наблюдение: отопление идёт по расписанию» below while the room does not follow it, and what occupancy would give a source «Конвектор: кабинет: по присутствию держал бы 19°» or «… по присутствию выключил бы» (`occupancy_dry_run`), with «Никого» or «Занято» below. The fireplace and learning read the same way: «Камин: топится до 22:10» (`fireplace_marked`) and «Камин: погас» (`fireplace_mark_cleared`), with «Из Telegram» below for a mark from the chat; «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: похоже, топится камин (+2,1°/ч)» with the fastest rise of the room's sensors (`external_heat_started`), «Наблюдение: обучение не останавливается» below while the fireplace's gate is off, and «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: похоже, камин прогорел» (`external_heat_ended`), with «Прошло 6 часов» below when the fire timed out; «Гостиная: утро не выучено: 3,4°/ч быстрее предела 1,5°/ч» (`zone_morning_rejected`); and «Гостиная: обучение сброшено» (`zone_learning_reset`) with what it had learned below, «Было: утр 4, задержка 35 мин, раннее отключение 0,25°». Each command the chat sent is journaled before it runs and reads as the owner wrote it, «Telegram: «холодно в гостиной»» (`owner_command`, section 9). A change made on a thermostat reads «Кухня: изменили на термостате: 28° → 25°, teplo не трогает до 18:40» (`zone_manual_change`), or «… teplo не трогал бы до 18:40» while its gate is off, with «выключили», «включили» or «режим hold → smart» for the power and the mode. A heat source's commands read «Конвектор: кабинет: поставил 22°», «… включил на 22°» or «… выключил» (`source_command_issued`), «… поставил бы 22°» in dry-run, a failed one «… не удалось поставить 22°» with its cause (`source_command_failed`), and a change made on the unit «Конвектор: кабинет: изменили на приборе: 22° → 25°, teplo не трогает до 18:40» (`source_manual_change`), with a mode in words, «режим обогрев → осушение», of «обогрев», «охлаждение», «авто» for `auto` and `heat_cool`, «осушение», «вентиляция», «другой режим» and «выкл», or «… не трогал бы …» while its commands do not reach it; each names the source as its subject. A climate unit's availability reads like the phase meter's, «Конвектор: кабинет: нет связи» and «… на связи», as its name says what it is, and so does a presence source's, «Компьютер: кабинет: нет связи».

Diagnostic text never reaches the page. A failure or an untrusted clock is explained by a translated message chosen by its code; a message that would only repeat its headline is left out, and an unknown code or a journal entry written before codes existed gets a generic one. The raw text stays in the element's tooltip, and for a device failure also in the journal payload and the log. The journal and these messages name the kind of device, as the alerts do: a thermostat, a plug or a sensor, while the phase meter's own name says what it is, e.g. «Фазометр: нет связи».

### Schedule and settings

The schedule page lists the rooms that hold comfort, by floor and then as the dashboard orders them, each with its floor named small and a row per weekday: a bar of the day in the regime colours of the chips, the periods in words, «Ночь 00:00–03:30 · Разогрев 03:30–06:30 · Комфорт 06:30–23:30 · Ночь 23:30–24:00», and the comfort windows. The periods come from the schedule resolution of floor mode (section 6) with the longest fixed pre-heat lead among the room's zones, so a warm-up before an early window starts the evening before and one after a short break starts when the window before it ends; a room without a zone draws no warm-up. Each room says when its zones start warming up: a room of one zone in one sentence, «Разогрев начинается за 3 ч до комфорта.», a room of several zones a line per zone, «Гостиная: разогрев начинается за 3 ч до комфорта.», a room heated by a source teplo commands names the source, «Конвектор: кабинет держит комфорт с начала окна, без разогрева.», as a fast source holds comfort from the start of a window with no lead (section 6, Fast sources), and once the room follows occupancy adds «Конвектор: кабинет следует присутствию: комфорт, пока здесь кто-то есть, и ночная цель, когда никого нет 20 мин; окна действуют, пока нет данных о присутствии.», and a room with neither «teplo пока нечем греть эту комнату по расписанию.». A zone under air control says that optimal start sets its warm-up from what the room has learned, at most the lead cap before comfort, and on the fixed lead until it has learned the mornings it needs. Optimal start caps every lead (section 6), so the page gives the fixed lead of such a zone at most the lead cap; the other zones, which floor mode drives, keep their whole fixed lead. A window is added, changed or deleted in its day, a day is copied to other days of its room and a room's week to other rooms; each replaces only the days that change. A window lies within one day on the 15-minute grid and ends after it starts, and it overlaps no other window of its day; the panel refuses anything else in words and writes nothing. A window entered off the grid in the admin stays until it is changed, and a day holding one is not copied.

The settings page lists the rooms that hold comfort, each room's card followed by the cards of its zones, and then the zones in no room climate. A room's card, headed by its name with its floor named small, edits whether it is a guest room, its comfort and night air targets and, for a room with backup heat, the threshold and hysteresis of backup heat; a zone's card edits its name, heating on or off, comfort, night, boost, idle and limit floor setpoints, for an air zone outside a room climate its comfort and night air targets, and its pre-heat lead; the climate card edits the climate settings' air start offset, initial early-stop offset, minimum band, learned mornings needed, pre-heat lead cap and the hour guests leave, 0–23; the load settings' start ceiling, shed threshold and its hold, restore threshold and its hold, start interval, meter-lost time and default load current; and each load's priority, phase and nominal current. Values stay within what the devices and the domain take: floor setpoints and air targets 5.0–40.0 °C in tenths, the thermostat's own range (DP 16, section 4), night at or below comfort, boost at or above it, idle below the limit, the night air target at or below the comfort one, backup heat at least 0.5 °C below the room's night target and a lead of 0–240 minutes; the offsets, the minimum band, the lead cap, the mornings and the backup hysteresis within the ranges of the climate settings, and the start offset at or above the minimum band, which the admin checks as well (section 6); currents of 0.1–63.0 A in tenths, holds and intervals of 1–3600 s, the restore threshold at or below the start ceiling and the start ceiling below the shed threshold, as the database keeps them (section 7). Every load keeps a priority of its own; the loads are saved together, so two loads swap their priorities in one save, and a load moved above the highest priority taken passes through a spare priority above every one taken or asked for. A refused value is explained at its field and nothing is written.

The gates stay in the admin. A zone shows its `live_control` and, for an air zone, its `air_control_live`, a room with backup heat its `backup_live`, a room with a fireplace the fireplace's `live` as «Детектор камина», a room with a heat source teplo commands the source's `live` under the source's name, «Конвектор: кабинет», a room with a presence source and no floor its `follows_occupancy` as «По присутствию» with «Никого — после 20 мин без присутствия» below, the climate card the climate settings' `manual_hold_live` as «Ручная правка на термостатах», the load settings `load_management_live` and each load its `live`, as «Работает» or «Наблюдение» with a link to its page in the admin; with `TEPLO_DRY_RUN` on, the page adds that nothing is sent. The panel's forms and handlers carry no gate, and a save writes only the fields that changed, never a gate, a boost, an override, a hold by hand, the guests' end, a write stamp, the estimated phase or what load management stored. Changes take effect through the usual paths, and the pages say so: the heating cycle reads the rooms, the zones and the climate settings every minute, a new setpoint or schedule reaches the thermostat at the zone's next allowed write within its 30-minute budget (section 4), and the load guard reads the loads and the load settings within a minute (section 7).

A save of a card, and an edit of the schedule, reads what is stored, checks the edit, works out which fields or days change, writes them and journals them in one database transaction, which takes the database's write lock with its first statement, before it reads. Two saves at once, from two phones or two tabs, therefore run one after the other: the second waits for the first, up to the 20 seconds a locked database is waited for, then reads what the first stored and checks and writes its own card against that, so every field its card shows that differs from what is stored is written, and no save ever stores a mix of two cards that neither checked. An edit of the schedule locks the room climates whose weeks it reads the same way, so a window added to a day keeps a window another edit added to that day a moment before, and a window whose day another edit replaced meanwhile is no longer found and nothing more is written. A database that stays locked beyond that, or refuses the write, saves nothing, and the card says «Не сохранено: база данных не приняла изменение. Попробуйте ещё раз» over the values as they were typed; the schedule says the same in the tool that sent the edit, over the times typed or the days and rooms picked, and for a deletion in the window, which stays open. The database itself keeps a zone's idle floor setpoint below its floor limit and the air start offset at or above the minimum band, the rules whose breach would turn air mode around, and a room's night target at or below its comfort target and its backup heat at least 0.5 °C below the night target, so neither a save nor the admin can store such a pair; the admin explains each rule on its page. Every saved change is journaled as `settings_changed`, one entry per changed field, in the same transaction as the change, so a change the journal refuses is not kept. The payload names the object (`room`, `zone`, `schedule`, `climate_settings`, `load_settings` or `load`), the room's, the zone's or the load's name, the field, and the old and new values; a schedule is the room's, its field is the weekday and the values list the day's windows. A room's entry is named by its payload, never by its slug, which a zone may share. The journal words them: «Спальня: воздух в комфорте 20,5° → 21°», «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая: теперь гостевая комната», «Климат: гости до 14:00 → 12:00», «Спальня: резервный обогрев ниже 19° → 18,5°», «Кухня: пол в комфорте 28° → 27,5°», «Гостиная, кухня и прихожая, понедельник: комфорт 06:30–23:30 → 07:00–23:30», «Кухня: отопление выключено», «Управление нагрузкой: порог отключения 24 А → 25 А» and «Бойлер 1 этаж: приоритет 1 → 2».

## 11. Configuration and reuse

- **Site app.** `sites/<name>/` is a Django app whose data migrations create zones, devices, schedules, tariffs, thresholds and the shedding priority, link the air zones to their air sensors, fill the climate settings, and create the floors and rooms, place each zone and device in its room and give each room its comfort and backup heat. The active site is selected by setting. `sites/example` is the template.
- **Estimated phase.** The load settings name a phase without a clamp, `estimated_phase` (blank, `l1`, `l2` or `l3`), and the base current of its estimate, `estimated_base_amps`, 0.0–63.0 A in tenths, 2.0 A by default (section 7). `sites/barsoom` sets `l2` and gives Water heater 2F and Bathroom 1F the phase `l2` where it was still unknown; `sites/example`, whose meter has every clamp, estimates nothing. Both fields are edited in the admin only (`/admin/power/loadsettings/`), so a save of the panel's load settings card never writes them.
- **Secrets file.** TOML on the router, default `/opt/etc/teplo/secrets.toml`:

```toml
[devices.thermostat-kitchen]
address = '192.0.2.10'
device_id = '...'
local_key = '...'
protocol = '3.3'

[devices.plug-boiler-1f]
address = '192.0.2.11'
device_id = '...'
local_key = '...'
protocol = '3.5'

[devices.heater-office]
address = '192.0.2.12'
device_id = '...'
local_key = '...'
protocol = '3.5'

[telegram]
token = '...'
chat_id = '...'
proxy = 'http://...'
commands = true

[panel]
password = '...'

[mqtt]
host = '192.0.2.1'
port = 1883
username = 'teplo'
password = '...'
```

`commands` is off unless it is `true`: then the chat also commands teplo (section 9). `port` defaults to 1883. Without the `[mqtt]` table, or with an empty `host`, `username` or `password`, or a port outside 1–65535, teplo runs without MQTT. The password never reaches the log.

## 12. Deployment

- **Entware packages:** python3, python3-pip, python3-sqlite3, python3-asyncio, python3-cryptography, mosquitto-nossl, and mosquitto-client-nossl for checking the broker.
- **Paths:** code `/opt/share/teplo`, settings and secrets `/opt/etc/teplo`, database `/opt/var/lib/teplo`, logs `/opt/var/log/teplo`.
- **Install:** a virtualenv with system site packages, so the Entware build of `cryptography` is reused.
- **Deploy:** from the workstation over SSH (port 222): ship the code, install dependencies, run migrations, restart the service.

## 13. Testing and rollout

- **Domain:** pure unit tests.
- **Use cases:** stub ports passed through constructors.
- **Infrastructure:** protocol encoding against captured frames; repositories against the test database with factories.
- **Views:** mocks.
- **Scenarios:** a house simulator (thermal model per zone, phase currents, EV and appliance events) drives the control loops through days of simulated time, e.g. "EV at 22 A, water heater and microwave on the same phase: no phase above 24 A for more than 3 s".
- **Architecture:** AST tests fail when a domain module imports Django, a driver or a network client, when any module but the shared Tuya client builder imports tinytuya, and when any module but the MQTT client imports paho.
- **CI:** GitHub Actions runs ruff, mypy, `makemigrations --check` and pytest on every push, and the office computer's script's tests again under Python 3.12, the computer's Python; any failure fails the build.
- **Rollout:** every stage first runs in dry-run, journalling the commands it would send, then is enabled zone by zone.

## 14. Roadmap

1. Thermostats in floor mode: schedules, night setback, timed pre-heat, boost, dry-run, dashboard and journal.
2. Phase meter and load management. Ships in dry-run: the guard watches the phases every second, learns the loads' phases, spots EV charging and journals the sheds, restores and waiting starts it would make, and the panel shows the shedding order and the EV badge, until the owner switches load management on in the admin, load by load (section 7). The active calibration comes later.
3. Air sensors, air regulation, learned optimal start and the second-floor guard, now the bedroom's backup heat (the sensors are already read and alert when lost). Ships in dry-run: the zones learn their mornings and the journal shows what air control, optimal start and backup heat would do, until the owner switches them on in the admin, air control zone by zone and backup heat on the bedroom's room climate (section 6).
4. Smart plugs: the water heater plugs switch through their own driver and are the first loads of the shedding order, in dry-run with stage 2 until they are switched on; washing machine monitoring.
5. Schedule and settings pages: the comfort windows and the daily settings are edited in the panel and journaled, while the gates stay in the admin (section 10).
6. House model in three blocks. Block A: floors and rooms, comfort per room, backup heat in place of the second-floor guard, guests, 2-hour room overrides and Telegram commands. Block B, shipped after it: an estimate of the unclamped phase L2, the fireplace and learning hygiene, the wall thermostats' 2-hour manual hold, and the office convector commanded as a fast heat source and shed as a load (sections 4, 6 and 7). Block C: presence and the bedroom AC. Its first part ships each room's occupancy from the presence sources placed in it, watched only until the owner ticks a room's `follows_occupancy` (section 6, see Occupancy), the office computer's script as the first source and the broker's ACL (section 4). Its second part ships the bedroom AC's driver, firmware and source rules, its outdoor lockout and modes, without a unit until its dongle answers (sections 4 and 6); the radars and a frost heater are planned in section 15. Each block ships with its gates off and has them switched on within days. The floors and rooms come first: the site data places every zone and device in a room, and the dashboard and `api/v1/rooms/` show the house by floor and room. Then each room holds its comfort windows and air targets, the schedule and settings pages work by room, and the second-floor guard becomes the bedroom's backup heat, while every zone decides as before. Then guests and 2-hour room overrides set a room's target, which the heating cycle, the panel and the zone-off alert read alike, and which reaches the floors only through their gates; without guests or an override every zone decides as before. Last, the owner's Telegram chat takes guests, room targets, boost and a status through the panel's use cases (section 9).

Later: heat pump driver, EV charger driver, away and frost modes, home or away read from the phones on the LAN, and summer cooling with the bedroom AC.

## 15. Radars and a frost heater (planned)

This section is the target design of what is left of block C. The sections above describe teplo as it runs, occupancy, the presence sources and the bedroom AC included (sections 4 and 6); block C updates them in the change that ships each part and leaves here only what is still planned.

- **Radars.** HLK-LD2410C mmWave radars, each on its room's air node: the office on a new ESP32 DevKit with an SHT30 (`teplo-air-office`); the living room on a DevKit with an SHT30 that replaces its SuperMini and keeps its node name; the bedroom on UART2 of the bedroom DevKit, `teplo-air-second-floor`. Each radar has Bluetooth off and its detection range limited to the room, and its node's page asks for a login. The radar publishes a template sensor `presence`, 1 or 0, every 30 seconds to `teplo/<node>/sensor/presence/state`, as ESPHome publishes a binary sensor only when it changes and a room occupied for an hour would fall silent. Each radar is added in the admin as a presence source of its room, `presence-office` with the node `air-office`, and teplo hears it after a restart (section 4). The first modules arrived with the wrong pin pitch, so the radars come later; the living room's radar drives nothing, as the open space heats with floors only.
- **Frost heater.** A frost heater for the bedroom is a second source of the room, chosen while the AC is locked out (section 6, see Fast sources); it is data only if it speaks `tuya_heater`'s data points.

The gate opener is a separate task with its own design; this section reserves no board, topic or view for it.
